The news: SpaceX is the largest company ever to list, and it lists as a broadband operator carrying two loss-making ventures. Space runs a $(2,168)M annualised operating loss and AI $(5,028)M. Connectivity is the only segment with a positive operating result — $1,656M in the quarter — and the whole $2.07 trillion is 312.8× that segment's annualised income.

Why that inverts the standard question. A sum-of-the-parts analysis normally asks whether the parts are worth more than the whole, and reports the gap as a conglomerate discount. That question is not available here. The parts do not sum to less than the whole under any admissible multiple — the whole already requires the one profitable segment to carry a ~313× operating multiple. What this means for the position is that the discount is not small, it is absent, and the live question becomes a different one: is the market crediting the two loss-making segments with any value at all?

The anchor's job is to bound that question rather than answer it. This thesis produces three valuation regimes — one per segment — each a MODELED range with its boundary named, and no blended multiple and no point estimate. It is the layer beneath the sector theses: 005, 006 and 009 each price a company that consumes what this anchor establishes.

And the anchor's most uncomfortable result is a refusal. The comparability partition admits zero of eleven named comparators — not a search that failed, but three structurally different reasons that one screen would not have found. A downstream thesis that borrows one of the eleven has re-opened a closed class without saying so, and the failure would be invisible because that thesis would look internally consistent.

How to read this report. The finding and why it survives every allocation come first, then the three boundaries, the segments one at a time, the capital structure and the market print — including two corrections this thesis made to its own artifacts while producing it. The recommendation is not a position. This thesis produces no trades by design. It produces the floor every downstream thesis stands on.

Why the ladder is the evidence rather than the 313×. A single attribution invites the reply that the analyst chose it. The ladder removes the choice: at every allocation a reader might defend, Connectivity carries a multiple with no terrestrial analogue. This means the conclusion is not an artefact of one attribution — the robustness across the range is the finding, which is why the anchor reports a ladder instead of a number.

Three of the four standard SOTP components do not exist here in the form the template assumes. The conglomerate discount cannot be measured as a discount and is reported as its inverse. The control and float discount is made harder by the Cursor consideration being Class A stock, so the two cannot be separated from the transaction the anchor now includes. Unallocated corporate cost is not separable — SPCX files no such line, and the anchor reports it as a bound rather than allocating it arbitrarily.

The +$534M is the segment's load-bearing number. It is what remains when Starship development is stripped out, and it establishes that launch carries standalone value rather than being a subsidised internal function. The limit travels with it: the filing does not split Starship from Falcon R&D, so the split is MODELED and the artifact says so.

Most of what Space does is never sold. Of 38 launches in Q2 2026, 10 carried a customer payload — 26.3%. The other 28 were internal Starlink deployments, and for those the issuer states: "the Company does not recognize any inter-segment revenue, rather those launch costs are capitalized in satellites in Property, plant, and equipment, net." This means the segment's revenue line measures a minority of its output, and Space's contribution to the group is capitalised into Connectivity's asset base — which is why 001's own finding was that value migrated out of launch. Launch counts: SPCX 10-Q p.35; the inter-segment rule: p.36.

Why no external multiple may be borrowed, stated as a measurement problem rather than a coverage one. A competitor's launch margin is a price against a cost. Space's is a residual customer book. They are not two measurements of one quantity — comparing them compares a price to a non-price. The value therefore comes from segment contribution, and the DA-06 flag travels with the row into every downstream thesis that cites it.

Connectivity · The Only Segment That Earns

Connectivity earns the profit, and its growth is a mix shift

Connectivity is the highest-margin operator in the universe and the only profitable SPCX segment. Its operating income grew faster than its revenue, which is the operating-leverage case demonstrated on the margin rather than argued.

The mix is moving toward the higher-value channel, and that is the growth story. Enterprise & Government — aviation, maritime, land mobility, fixed sites and government entities — grew 2.4× faster than consumer, both figures filed as dimensions of the same axis. This means the extension is a mix shift rather than a rising tide, and it is the more durable of the two: a managed contract is a relationship, not a consumer price point.

And the ARPU decline is real — but ARPU is not the segment's monetisation metric, and the two facts are different. The issuer states the cause directly: consumer revenue grew with "101.2% growth in Starlink subscribers, offset by a 22.4% decline in Starlink subscriber ARPU, primarily due to international expansion and the addition of lower priced service plans." That is a genuine mix shift toward cheaper international plans, and the report does not discount it. What ARPU cannot measure is the rest of the segment: its denominator "does not include managed enterprise and government customers", so it excludes the channel growing at +108.3%. This means two separate findings, kept separate — the consumer price decline is real, and ARPU is structurally blind to the fastest-growing part of Connectivity. DA-10 is registered because a service-line ARPU is not a company ARPU. Cause: SPCX 10-Q p.43; definition: p.36.

The definition excludes exactly the costs a valuation would need. Nameplate compute draw is IT load — it "does not include power we install and use for our supporting infrastructure such as cooling systems, power distribution losses, lighting, security systems, or facility-level overhead." This means the AI segment is carried at INVESTED CAPITAL — a cost basis — and that is a constitutional bound rather than an analytical gap, the only basis the disclosure supports. What it costs the reader: there is no AI valuation in this thesis, only a bound.

What it means for the downside: at this coverage the equity is not funding growth from operations, so the price of the next dollar is set by the capital markets rather than by the business.

And at this moment the constraint binds through one disclosure, which is now resolved. The Cursor acquisition is complete — not pending. The Reverse-DCF · What the Price Requires, Solved Backwards

The live price requires perpetual growth equal to the required return

A forward DCF is not admissible here — consolidated free cash flow is negative and fails the programme's ≥3-year positive-FCF limb. The reverse-DCF is admissible precisely because it forecasts nothing: it solves for the growth a price implies and reports it.

g ≈ r − 0.32% is the whole result in one line. At every point in the constitution's own 15–25% band, the growth the price implies is essentially the required return itself; when g approaches r, terminal value approaches the entire present value, and almost none of the $2.07 trillion pays for cash flows anyone can currently see. This breaches F1's own terminal-value cap, and the anchor reports three readings rather than resolving them — the price may be too high; the cap may be the wrong instrument at this stage of a capital-intensive build; or the attribution may be the artefact, since the 313× sets Space and AI to zero by construction. What this means for the position: it produces neither a long nor a short thesis, but the demand the price makes on the business — that Connectivity compound at the cost of capital, indefinitely, with the other two segments contributing nothing.

Seven triggers carry the anchor forward, each with a datum and a threshold rather than a direction — so a downstream thesis can check whether the anchor has gone stale without re-running it. Every one of the four rests on a refusal as much as a finding — and the anchor's refusal is that the sector's benchmark company has no admissible comparators, so its value must be bounded rather than measured.

The recommendation is not a position. This thesis produces no trades by design, and its deliverable is a citation rule: carry the basis with the grade, and carry the boundary with the row.
