## Key arguments

**A1 — The anchor's whole price sits on one segment, and the arithmetic says so.**
SPCX's live market capitalisation is **$2.0718T** (13,567,041,680 shares × **$152.71**, close
2026-09-18). Connectivity's annualised operating income is **$6,624M**. The ratio is **312.8×** —
**and Connectivity is the only profitable segment.** Space runs **$(2,168)M** annualised and AI
**$(5,028)M**. **This means** the standard SOTP question — do the parts exceed the whole? — is not
the live question. **The live question is whether the market credits the two loss-making segments
at all.**

**A2 — And the finding survives every allocation, which is what makes it a finding.**
Even granting Space and AI **$1.5T** — 72% of the market cap for two loss-making segments —
Connectivity still carries **86×**. At **$0.5T** it carries **237×**. **This means** the conclusion
is not an artefact of attributing the whole price to one segment; it holds across the plausible
range, and the *robustness* is the evidence.

**A3 — The three regimes have no comparators, and they fail for three DIFFERENT reasons.**
The partition admits **0 of 11**. Space fails on **earnings** (RKLB and FLY are `loss_making`, and
DA-06 means Space has **no transaction price** at all). Connectivity fails on **price formation**
(IRDM and GSAT are `P11` — the price is a spread, so **a live multiple on a spread is no more
admissible than a stale one**). AI fails on **disclosure** (MSFT's and GOOG's compute are **cost
centres**, not segments). **This means** a single comp screen would not have found them, and any
downstream thesis that borrows a comparator has re-opened a closed class.

**A4 — The AI segment cannot be valued from public disclosure, and that is a constitutional bound
rather than an analytical gap.** Its headline metric, **1.4 GW**, is **IT load** (DA-11) —
explicitly excluding cooling, distribution losses, lighting, security and facility overhead. It is
a **capacity** figure, and no disclosed `$/kW` converts it. **This means** P3's headline is
**invested capital** — a **cost** basis — and the segment is carried as a bound, not a value.

**A5 — The growth is a mix shift, not a rising tide, and the mix is moving the right way.**
Enterprise & Government grew **+108.3%** against Consumer's **+44.4%** — **2.4× faster**, both
filed dimensions. Connectivity's operating income rose **+79.4%** on revenue **+65.8%**. **This
means** the operating-leverage case is demonstrated on the margin, **while ARPU fell −22.4%** —
and the ambiguity between price erosion and mix shift is a **bound carried**, not resolved.

**A6 — The anchor is buying time with capital, and `CAPITAL` is the binding constraint.**
Operating cash flow covers **0.1005×** of the investing outflow — an **order of magnitude** from
the 1.0× falsifier. **$85,675M** of IPO equity and **$40,869M** of notes fund a build that names
**data centers before launch facilities**. **This means** the downside is governed by the funding
structure, and at this moment it binds through **one disclosure** — the Cursor dilution, now
**resolved at 391,041,680 shares**.

**A7 — The capital goes to the segment that loses the most, and the filings say so in one table.**
AI took **$15,828M** of **$18,369M** quarterly capex — **86.2%** — while running the largest
operating loss and producing **32.8%** of revenue. Connectivity, the only profitable segment,
received **7.4%**. **This means** the allocation is not a rounding question but a direction, and it
is the same direction as the Cursor acquisition: **capital is moving toward the AI adjacency and
away from the business that currently earns.** *(This argument exists because a bound was
RETRACTED — see Evidence 4.)*

## Story line

**A single company that dominates its sector, priced entirely on the one part of itself that
works.** The reader arrives knowing SpaceX as a launch company. The report shows the launch
business is **12.3% of revenue and fell 1.9% across a half in which consolidated revenue rose
53.7%** — and that the **$2.07 trillion** price is **312.8× the operating income of the broadband
segment**, with the other two segments running losses.

**Then it does the harder thing: it shows why that is not a mispricing claim.** The anchor
**admits no comparator** — not because the search failed but because **comparability fails
structurally, three different ways**. The AI segment's own headline metric **cannot legally be
converted into a value under A4 and DA-11**. The launch business has **no transaction price at
all**.

**And it ends where an anchor should: with boundaries, not a number.** Seven triggers with datums
and thresholds. Five items it could not value, each with the disclosure that would resolve it. An
explicit statement of what 005, 006 and 009 may price off — and that **no downstream thesis may
borrow a comparator this anchor excluded**, because that failure would be invisible in a thesis
that looked internally consistent.
