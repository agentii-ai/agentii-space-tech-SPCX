# RKLB × risk — the acquirer's tag means the opposite thing

## The finding

**`deal_security_basis: standalone_pre_merger` is mandatory on all three P11 securities, and at
RKLB it means the inverse of what it means at IRDM and GSAT.** At the two targets, the standalone
business is **contractually ceasing to exist** — its operating metrics are a description of a
company that will not survive the close. At RKLB, the acquirer, the standalone business
**continues**; what happens is that the filed risk-factor set is a description of a company that
is about to be **transformed**. Same tag, same legal basis, opposite mechanism, and a reader who
learns the tag at IRDM will misapply it here.

The practical consequence: **every risk factor in RKLB's 24-page Item 1A (10-K p.16–39) is
pre-close calibratable but post-close non-transferable.** The filing's own risk set does not
contain the merger — it is a standalone risk set — while the merger is the largest single
change in the issuer's risk profile. The merger risk block appears **only in the Q2 2026 10-Q**
(p.48–51).

**And RKLB is the only issuer in this universe where PIL-6's falsifier is REACHABLE.** §6 is the
census; the headline is that the cost curve, correctly measured on RKLB's own filed mix,
**inverts**: cost per launch fell **12.0%** while cost per kg to LEO rose **32.0%**.

## 3. Mode — technology-disruption-risk-analysis

**RKLB is the only issuer in this universe that files the mission mix, and the mix is where the
technology risk actually sits.**

**The HASTE disclosure, verbatim and load-bearing:** *"Two of the six Electron launch missions
completed for the three months ended June 30, 2026 were Hypersonic Accelerator Suborbital Test
Electron ('HASTE') launch missions, for which revenue was recognized over time and was partially
recognized in prior quarters. All five Electron launch missions completed for the three months
ended June 30, 2025 were point-in-time launches."*
`[📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37)`

Two distinct technical risks are named in one sentence, and the register conflates them:

1. **A suborbital mission inside a launch count.** 2 of 6 Q2 2026 "launch missions" carried
   **zero kilograms to low Earth orbit** while sitting inside both the revenue numerator and the
   launch-count denominator.
2. **An over-time recognition basis inside a point-in-time series.** HASTE revenue is recognised
   **over time** and **"partially recognized in prior quarters"** — so a revenue figure attributed
   to Q2 2026 on a launch-count basis contains revenue earned in earlier periods. **This is DA-30
   as a live technology-disruption measurement problem**: two recognition bases on one revenue
   concept, and the filer does not relate them.

**Consequence for the cost curve.** Revenue per launch 3M **$9.1M (2026) vs $7.9M (2025)** and
cost per launch **$4.4M vs $5.0M (−12.0%)**. Taken at face value the curve falls. On the corrected
mass denominator it rises:

| Basis | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missions | 5 of 5 point-in-time, **0 HASTE** | 6, **2 HASTE**, 4 orbital | — |
| Mass to LEO (× 300 kg ceiling) | **300 kg** | **200 kg** | **−33.3%** |
| Cost per launch (as filed) | $5.0M | $4.4M | **−12.0%** |
| **Cost per kg to LEO** (as filed) | **$16,667/kg** | **$22,000/kg** | **+32.0%** |
| Revenue per kg to LEO | $26,333/kg | $45,500/kg | +72.8% |
| Restatement factor vs the 300 kg ceiling | **1.00×** | **1.50×** | — |
| H1 2026 (≥10 of 12 orbital, 250 kg) | — | $36,800/kg rev; $19,600/kg cost; **≥1.20×** | — |

**🚨 The disclosed improvement is a denominator artefact in the OPTIMISTIC direction, and it is
this artifact's central result.** Cost per launch fell **12.0%** while cost per kg to LEO **rose
32.0%**, because two of six missions bought no kilogram. **The curve's only measured point,
correctly measured, moves the WRONG WAY.** `[📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37)`

**What must NOT be inherited, corrected.** The widely-quoted *"RKLB's $/kg is 1.79×–2.62× too
low, four of four periods"* is a **CROSS-ISSUER FUSION**: `1.79×` and `2.62×` are **Falcon 9's**
realized-denominator factors (22.8 ÷ 12.76 and 22.8 ÷ 8.70) computed from **SPCX's** filed
mass-to-orbit table, while *"four of four periods"* is **Electron's** DA-25 period count.
**RKLB files no mass-to-orbit metric in ANY period**, so the SPCX-style restatement cannot be
reproduced here. **What IS reproducible at RKLB is 1.00×–1.50× (+0% to +50%), driven by the HASTE
mission mix — and the correction is ZERO in the one period whose mix is fully filed (Q2 2025,
1.00×).** *"Four of four"* is FALSIFIED.

**A hard bound that must travel with every one of these numbers.** Electron is filed **only** as
*"up to 300 kg"* — **a CEILING, not a payload.** Every $/kg figure above is therefore an **upper
bound on cost per kg and a lower bound on mass**, and the true denominators are **≤200 kg**, so
the true cost per kg is **≥$22,000**. The resolving disclosure is **an Electron mass-to-orbit
metric on a stated definition** — `UNRESOLVABLE-FROM-PUBLIC-SOURCES`, and the only item in this
artifact that would convert the curve's single measured point from a bound into a value.
**Q1 2025, H1 2025 and Q1 2026 mission mixes are not filed → `UNEXERCISED`, never folded in.**

**Neutron is F5b — partially reusable — and UNFLOWN.** `[📄 RKLB 10-K p.20](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/20)`
Electron is **F5c — fully expendable — and it is the ONLY architecture in the universe with a
DEMONSTRATED price**; recovery is filed only as a *"potential ability … providing a path to"*
reusability, which is a statement about an intention, not an architecture.
`[📄 RKLB 10-K p.24](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/24)` **The three-way split F5a/F5b/F5c is
exhaustive, and the only DEMONSTRATED tier is the one with no reuse at all.** Cost overruns,

basis is the same — the filed statements are pre-close. **The risk consequence is not: for RKLB
the merger is the largest single addition to the risk profile and it appears nowhere in the
24-page risk set; it appears only in the Q2 2026 10-Q.** A reader who checks "does the 10-K
disclose the merger's risks?" finds a generic acquisitions paragraph on p.35 and concludes
inadequate disclosure. **The disclosure is adequate; it is in a different filing.**
