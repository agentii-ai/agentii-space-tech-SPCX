# RKLB × operational-kpi — the per-launch series, and its failure to reconcile to the audited segment table

## 0. The finding

**The `revenue per launch` / `cost per launch` series does not reconcile to the audited segment
table, in most periods, on either leg — and the `× mission-count` construction that would make
it do so is `QUARANTINED`. It FAILS the mandatory stability test.**

The brief's headline reconciliation reproduces arithmetically: `$9.1M × 6 = $54,600k` against a
filed Launch Services revenue of `$44,586k` is a **+22.46%** overstatement
[📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37) ·
[📄 RKLB 10-Q p.32](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/32). That is not the finding. The finding is
that **this construction closes in some periods and fails in others, changes the sign of its own
error twice inside four quarters, and has never closed outside the metric's own rounding band
after Q1 2025.** Under the brief's own rule — *a construction that closes at one period and fails
at the next is a coincidence, not a mechanism* — the construction is **not admissible as a
derivation of the audited segment table.**

**Corrections to claims carried into this task, established here by arithmetic on the filings:**

It widens on **every like-for-like comparison except Q1**, and on every annual comparison (§1.3) |

**The mechanism is period-normalisation, and `timing` is NOT reinstated.** The construction was
tested against the one competing basis the filings permit — a point-in-time-only scope, which the
Q2 2025 10-Q states in its own words — and it **fails on that basis too** (+44.82% in Q2 2026,
+18.37% in H1 2026). No single basis closes the series. That, and not a recognition lag, is what
makes this a normalisation (§5).

**One item is `UNRESOLVABLE-FROM-PUBLIC-SOURCES`, and the frontmatter flag scopes to it alone:**
the metric's own numerator-and-denominator construction is never disclosed (§16). **The `×N`
falsifier WAS exercised and it FAILED — flagging the construction as unresolvable does not soften
that result.** A construction whose terms cannot be located is uninformative whether or not it
closes; this one fails on the arithmetic as well.

## 1. The series as disclosed — every period available

### 1.1 The metric as filed, with its definitions

The metric is issuer-defined, disclosed in the MD&A key-metrics block, and stated to the nearest
`$0.1M`. **Its definition is not stable across filings** — three distinct wordings are filed:

| Filing | Filed wording of the numerator |
|---|---|
| Q1 2025 10-Q [📄 p.27](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec101/27) | "**Revenue value per launch** represents the average **revenue per launch contract** attributable to **launches that occurred** during a period, **regardless of when the revenue was recognized**." |
| Q2 2025 10-Q [📄 p.30](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec102/30) | "Revenue value per launch represents the average revenue per launch contract attributable to **point-in-time launches** that occurred during a period." ← **narrowest** |
| Q3 2025 10-Q onward — [📄 sec103 p.31](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec103/31) · [📄 sec104 p.31](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec104/31) · [📄 sec109 p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37) · FY2025 10-K [📄 p.46](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/46) | "Revenue per launch represents the average **transaction price** attributable to **launch contract performance obligations during the period in which the launch occurs**, regardless of whether the revenue is recognized using the point-in-time or over-time method of revenue recognition." |

**The cost leg's construction is filed, and it is not the segment's cost line.** Q2 2026, verbatim:
*"Cost per launch is calculated by taking **actual costs of the launch vehicles that occur in the
period**, regardless of whether the costs were recognized using the point-in-time or over-time
method and **all period costs in the period of launch**."*
[📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37). *"All period costs"* is defined nowhere —
it is not the Launch Services cost of revenues line, and §4.2 measures the difference. The Q2 2025
10-Q [📄 p.30](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec102/30) files a revenue-leg definition and no cost-leg
definition.

Two things follow, and both are load-bearing. **(a)** The noun changed: `revenue per launch contract`
(Q1 2025) became `transaction price attributable to launch contract performance obligations` (Q3 2025
onward). A transaction price is a contract value; it has no reason to equal a period's recognised
revenue and is dimensionally free to exceed it — which, at Q2 2026, it does (§4.4). **(b)** The cost
leg is defined explicitly in Q1 2025 and from Q3 2025 onward, but **Q2 2025's page defines only the
revenue leg** — the cost-per-launch basis is unstated in that filing
[📄 RKLB 10-Q p.30](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec102/30).

The issuer's own caution is filed and is repeated verbatim in every period: the metric
*"may not provide absolute clarity with regards to pricing and competitive dynamics in the
marketplace"*, and its variability drivers are enumerated as *"unique orbit and insertion
requirements, payload handling needs, launch location, time sensitivity of mission completion,
**method of revenue recognition** and other factors"*
[📄 RKLB 10-K p.46](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/46). That list is a disclosure about the
metric's construction, not an explanation of any gap asserted here.

### 1.3 The spread: corrected

`Rev/launch − cost/launch`, chronological, `$M`: `0.1 → 2.1 → 1.7 → 2.0 → 2.0 → 2.1 → 1.4 → 2.9 →
2.2 → 4.2 → 3.7 → 3.9 → 4.7 → 4.3`.

- **Not monotone.** It narrows at Q1 2024 → Q2 2024 (`2.1 → 1.7`). The apparent narrowings at
  `Q2 2025 → H1 2025` (`2.9 → 2.2`) and `Q3 2025 → FY2025` (`4.2 → 3.7`) are **period-length
  artefacts** — the longer period *contains* the shorter one, so those two pairs are not
  comparable and I do not count them either way.
- **Widens on every like-for-like comparison, with one exception.** Q2: `1.7 → 2.9 → 4.7`;
  H1: `2.0 → 2.2 → 4.3`; Annual: `0.1 → 2.1 → 3.7`. **Q1 is the exception**: `2.1 → 1.4 → 3.9` —
  it narrows in 2025 before widening in 2026.
- The brief's `$1.4M → $4.7M` is a Q1 2025 → Q2 2026 span across five intervening periods. As a
  **like-for-like year-over-year** statement it is correct and durable; as a monotonicity claim it
  is not.
- **The widening is driven by both legs, not by price alone.** Revenue per launch rose
  `$7.1M → $9.1M` (`+28.2%`) between Q1 2025 and Q2 2026 while cost per launch *fell*
  `$5.7M → $4.4M` (`−22.8%`). The cost leg contributes more of the widening than the revenue leg
  does in Q2 2026 (`$1.3M` of the `$3.3M` increase, on a same-period-length comparison).
- **Attribution is the issuer's, and it is not a list-price claim.** Filed verbatim: *"The increase
  in revenue per launch reflects changes in customer mix and mission complexity during the period in
  which the launches occurred."* [📄 sec109 p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37) ·
  [📄 sec104 p.31](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec104/31). **CLAIMED.** No list-price increase is
  asserted by the issuer, and none is asserted here.

### 1.4 Internal consistency of the annual point — a new check

The FY2025 10-K gives an annual metric and the 10-Qs give three quarters of the same year. If the

a genuine step-change in Q4 2025 that the company never disclosed in a quarterly filing, or a
metric whose period constructions are **not additive**. I cannot separate them, because **no Q4
10-Q exists** — and that is itself the point: this check runs entirely on filed cells, assumes
nothing about the denominator beyond the filed mission count, and still fails to close. It is
independent corroboration of §4.

   The `+0.25% / +0.24% / −0.46% / −0.26%` revenue-leg "closes" are all `≤ 0.7×` the band: they are

   exactly the failure mode DA-29 registers.

5. **The 5% threshold is not discriminable on the metric's own noise.** The metric's own
   period-to-period movement reaches `+27.9%` (rev/launch, Q2 2025 → Q3 2025) and `+18.0%`
   (cost/launch, same pair) — `3.6×` to `5.6×` the 5% criterion. A criterion smaller than the
   metric's ordinary quarterly movement cannot separate signal from movement. **The brief's
   `4 of 6` count is right for the revenue leg (`−0.26 / −15.32 / −8.80 / −12.35 / +22.46 / +1.99`),
   not the cost leg (`3 of 6`: `−22.90 / −12.83 / −8.58`).**

### 4.5 Scope impossibility at Q2 2026 — the sharpest single result

At Q2 2026 the construction gives `$54,600k` against **total** Launch Services revenue of

total.** Therefore **no scope re-basing whatever** — point-in-time only, over-time only, orbital
only, HASTE-excluded — can reconcile Q2 2026. The numerator is not a period revenue at all.

**And the comparator is the more generous one available.** The Launch Services revenue line is not
mission-only. Q2 2026, verbatim: the decline was *"partially offset by increased other launch
revenue of $5.7 million, which includes contract termination and study revenue"*; for the six
months: *"an increase in other launch revenue of $7.3 million, which includes contract termination
and study revenue and revenue recognized on over-time HASTE launch missions"*

[📄 RKLB 10-Q p.42](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/42). Both are **year-over-year deltas**, so
neither yields a level to subtract — but together they establish that the audited segment line
**contains revenue attaching to no Electron mission at all**. A mission-only construction therefore
has *less* claim on that line than a total-revenue construction does, and its `+$10,014k` overshoot
is not reducible to a scope mismatch operating in the issuer's favour.

The
issuer's own wording already says so: since Q3 2025 the metric is *"the average **transaction
price** attributable to launch contract performance obligations"*
[📄 sec103 p.31](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec103/31) ·
[📄 sec109 p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37) — a **contract value**, which is free to
exceed a period's recognised revenue and does.

## 5. The mechanism is period-normalisation — and `timing` stays falsified

**The brief's falsification of the timing hypothesis is respected and NOT re-opened.** Q2 2025 is
the zero-HASTE control period — *"All five Electron launch missions completed for the three months
ended June 30, 2025 were point-in-time launches."* [📄 sec109 p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37)
— and the construction still diverges there by `−15.32%` (revenue) and `−22.90%` (cost). **With no
HASTE missions in the period, a timing lag cannot be the mechanism, and nothing here attributes any
gap to one.**

**What the mechanism is.** A **period-normalisation**: a per-period average, defined by the issuer,
whose numerator is stated as a transaction price rather than a recognised revenue, whose denominator
the issuer never states, and which is never reconciled to the segment table by the issuer. Four filed
facts establish it, each independently of any hypothesis:

1. **The metric's scope is re-defined between filings** — three wordings, dated in §1.1, including
   one (`point-in-time launches`, Q2 2025) that is strictly narrower than the rest. A quantity whose
   definition is amended across the series is a normalisation, not a derivation.
2. **Its numerator is a transaction price from Q3 2025 onward** (§4.5), which is why it can exceed
   total segment revenue — a defining feature of a normalisation and an impossibility for a revenue
   derivation.
3. **Its denominator is unstated** (§2), so the `×N` assignment is an assumption made by the reader,
   not a term filed by the issuer.
4. **The issuer itself lists "method of revenue recognition" among the factors that make the metric
   vary** [📄 10-K p.46](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/46), and cautions that it *"may not provide
   absolute clarity with regards to pricing and competitive dynamics"*. This is the issuer's
   description of its own construction. It is not an explanation of any specific gap, and I do not
   use it as one.

### 5.1 The issuer's own "revenue recognition timing" language — and why timing is still not the mechanism

The Q2 2026 filing does use the phrase, and it must be distinguished rather than omitted: launch
revenue *"declined despite six Electron launch missions completed for the three months ended June
30, 2026 versus five Electron launch missions completed for the three months ended June 30, 2025,
primarily due to revenue recognition timing"* [📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37).
**That is a statement about the year-over-year change in the Launch Services segment revenue line**
— which fell from `$46,646k` to `$44,586k` on one additional mission — **not about the metric and
not about the `×N` construction**, which in the same period **overstates by `+$10,014k`**. The two
run in opposite directions: a recognition lag *lowers* a period's recognised revenue, while the
construction *raises* the period above the segment total (§4.5). And the zero-HASTE control period
has no over-time launch revenue for a lag to operate on at all. **The timing attribution stays
falsified as the explanation of the reconciliation gap, and the issuer's own use of the phrase
elsewhere does not disturb that.**

**Consequence for the thesis.** The cost-side claim survives as a **direction** but not as a
**measurement**: cost per launch falls `$7.0M → $4.4M` (`FY2023 → Q2 2026`, `−37%`) on the issuer's
own series — but that series fails the stability test against the audited table in 8 of 14 periods,
so **its level may not be quoted as the Launch Services segment's cost, and its slope may not be
quoted as a margin bridge.** The only quantity here that is quoted as a segment-level fact is the
audited table itself.

3. **Period labels are filed captions.** No Q-label is inferred and no annual figure is placed in a

4. **Every operating-income figure is the filed negative**, established through the component

7. **The stability test is reported in full, including the periods that pass** — a partial report of

8. **Rounding is propagated, not ignored.** Because the multiplicand is filed to `$0.1M`, the

## 15. Mode: `retrieval-strategy` — the retrieval order that produced this artifact

The sequence that works for an issuer-defined operational metric, in order, with the reason each
step is where it is:

1. **Documents first** (`search_documents` / `search_sec_filings`) to fix the accession set and the
   `citation_id`s. **Never** start from the extracted metrics block.
2. **The MD&A key-metrics page of the most recent 10-Q**, in full. This is where the metric, its
   definition, and the mission counts all live — **and where the definition can be compared to the
   same page in the prior year's filings**, which is the only way §1.1's basis change surfaces. Two
   adjacent filings sufficed to find three wordings.
3. **The segment note of the same filing, in full.** The audited comparator, read on the same day
   from the same document, so that no intervening extraction step can alter either side.
4. **The revenue-recognition-model note** (`sec109` p.11). This is the highest-value page in the set
   and is easy to miss: it is filed as a *revenue* note, not a segment note, and it is the only
   place the point-in-time / over-time **split by segment** appears. Without it, §4.4 cannot run at
   all and the competing basis stays invisible.
5. **The statement of operations face**, for every period, to anchor the operating-loss signs and the
   opex composition before quoting any of them (§9).
6. **Assemble the denominator from the cadence sentences across filings**, including the build-rate
   sentences — the counts are stated in prose, in different sentences from the metric, and the
   orbital/suborbital composition is disclosed only in the *revenue-growth* paragraph, not in the
   metric paragraph.
7. **Run the stability test across every period, both legs, before writing a single sentence about
   the reconciliation.** The test comes before the conclusion, not after it.

**Traps that this sequence caught, and that a metrics-block-first approach would have missed:**
the basis change between filings (step 2); the point-in-time comparator (step 4); the DA-23 strip,
which is invisible in the served layer and visible on the statement face (step 5); the suborbital
composition of the denominator, which is stated three paragraphs away from the metric (step 6); and
the sign instability of the error, which only appears once the test is run over the whole series
rather than at the reported period (step 7). **A single-period reconciliation would have reported
`+22.46%` and stopped. The finding is the other thirteen periods.**
