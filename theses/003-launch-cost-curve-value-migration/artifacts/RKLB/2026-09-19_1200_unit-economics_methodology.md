# RKLB × unit-economics — DA-01 A/A′/B/C, the F5c floor, and the RECONVERSION of the measured point

**RKLB is the curve's only measured point. It is also a `P11` deal security — `deal_security_basis:
standalone_pre_merger`, RKLB as acquirer of Iridium (announced 2026-06-29, close expected
mid-2027). Every figure below is pre-merger standalone and none of it absorbs Iridium.**

## 0. The finding

**001's published Electron figures — basis A `$30,333/kg` and basis B `$14,667/kg` — are not
wrong arithmetic. They are a construction on a CEILING, and the ceiling is the only payload mass
RKLB files. They are therefore FLOORS of the true $/kg, and the register's "1.79×–2.62× too low,
four of four periods" is the wrong magnitude and the wrong period count.**

Three results, in order of load:

1. **The RECONVERSION.** The filing files exactly one payload mass — *"launch services into low
   earth orbit on Electron for spacecraft up to 300 kg"* and *"capable of deploying spacecraft of
   up to 300 kg to low Earth orbit across a wide range of orbital inclinations from 38 to 120
   degrees from our operational LC-1 … and … 38 to 60 degrees from our operational LC-2"*
   ([📄 RKLB 10-K p.7](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/7);
   [📄 RKLB 10-K p.8](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/8)) — and it is a **ceiling**, twice stated as
   `up to`. RKLB files **no mass-to-orbit metric in any period**. The one realized-denominator
   adjustment the filing *does* support is the **HASTE mission mix**: two of the six Electron
   missions completed in Q2 2026 were Hypersonic Accelerator Suborbital Test Electron missions
   ([📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37)), which deliver **zero kg to LEO**
   while sitting inside both the revenue and the launch-count numerator. Excluding them from the
   kilogram denominator only:

   | Period | denominator | basis A (revenue) | basis B (cost) | reconv. factor |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | **Q2 2026** | 4 orbital of 6 × 300 kg = **200 kg** | **$45,500/kg** | **$22,000/kg** | **1.50×** |
   | Q2 2026, ceiling (001's inherited value) | 6 × 300 kg = 1,800 kg | $30,333/kg | $14,667/kg | 1.00× |
   | **H1 2026** | ≥10 orbital of 12 × 300 kg = **250 kg** | **$36,800/kg** | **$19,600/kg** | **≥1.20×** |
   | H1 2026, ceiling | 12 × 300 kg = 3,600 kg | $30,667/kg | $16,333/kg | 1.00× |
   | **Q2 2025** | 5 of 5 point-in-time, **0 HASTE** = **1,500 kg** | **$26,333/kg** | **$16,667/kg** | **1.00×** |
   | Q1 2025, H1 2025, Q1 2026 | mission mix **not filed** | — | — | **UNEXERCISED** |

   **The filing-supported reconversion is 1.00×–1.50× (+0% to +50%), not 1.79×–2.62× (+79% to
   +162%).** And it is **zero in the one period whose mix is fully filed** — Q2 2025 was *"All
   five … point-in-time launches"* with no HASTE at all — so **"four of four periods" is
   falsified**: the correction applies to **one of the three periods with a filed mix**, and is
   `UNEXERCISED` in the other three.

2. **The registered band's arithmetic is not RKLB's.** `22.8 t ÷ 8.70 t = 2.621` (+162.1%) and
   `22.8 t ÷ 12.76 t = 1.787` (+78.7%) are **Falcon 9's** realized-denominator ratios — 22.8 t is
   Falcon 9's nameplate capacity, and 8.70 t / 12.76 t are SPCX's filed t-per-launch from the
   8-K Space table ([📄 SPCX 10-Q p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35);
   [📄 SPCX 8-K p.7](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec7/7)). 002's own ledger states the +79%/+162%
   range against those two SPCX pages. **The direction transfers to RKLB; the magnitude does
   not, and 003 entities.md line 53 carries the range on row 26's attribution. This artifact
   reports the row as a mis-attribution of an SPCX ratio range onto an RKLB artifact.**

3. **DA-25 is quarantine-grade on BOTH legs — and it is period-normalisation, not timing.** The
   `×N` reconstruction (disclosed per-launch × launch count) closes in **2 of the 6 periods** and
   its residual **changes sign** — negative in every 2025 period, positive in every 2026 period —
   which no `timing` shift can produce. The zero-HASTE control kills `timing` outright: at Q2
   2025, where recognition period ≡ launch period by construction for all five missions, the
   revenue leg still diverges **−15.3%** and the cost leg **−22.9%**.

**And the inversion that matters for the thesis: on the corrected denominator, RKLB's
demonstrated unit cost ROSE. Cost per launch fell 12.0% YoY ($5.0M → $4.4M per launch), but cost
per kilogram to LEO rose from $16,667/kg to $22,000/kg — +32.0% — because two of the six Q2 2026
missions bought no kilogram at all.** The disclosed improvement is a denominator artefact; the
corrected series goes the other way.

### 3.4 DA-25, leg 1 — the revenue normalisation, and the stability test

**The stability test, run in all six periods, exactly as the brief requires: it is QUARANTINED.**

- **It closes (within ±5%) in 2 of 6 periods** — Q1 2025 (−0.26%) and H1 2026 (+1.99%).
- **It breaches ±15% in 2 of 6** — Q2 2025 (−15.32%) and **Q2 2026 (+22.46%)**, the registered
  "22.5% gap" — and ±5% in **4 of 6**.
- **The residual changes SIGN.** Negative in all three 2025 rows, positive in both 2026 quarters.
  **A construction that closes at one period and fails at the next is a coincidence, not a
  mechanism**, and here it does worse than fail — it reverses.

**And the sign reversal is falsified by a filed directional statement, not merely by tolerance.**
The only filed term the residual could be is *other launch revenue* — the disclosed metric is
*"the average transaction price attributable to launch contract performance obligations"*, which
by construction excludes it. The filing says of that very term, in the very quarter the residual
needs it to be **−$10,014k**:

> *"The decrease was partially offset by **increased** other launch revenue of $5.7 million, which
> includes contract termination and study revenue."*
> — [📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37)

**It cannot both increase by $5.7M and be −$10.0M.** The construction is refuted by a filed
statement of direction, and the refutation is independent of any tolerance choice.

**A second, independent refutation, in the opposite direction.** The same page files the YoY
Launch Services movement as a **decrease of $2.1M** in launch revenue, of which other launch
revenue contributed +$5.7M — so launch-contract revenue **fell $7.8M** YoY. The `×N` construction
requires it to have **risen $15.1M** ($39.5M → $54.6M). **A $22.9M YoY discrepancy, signed

**The mechanism is period-normalisation.** The disclosed metric's numerator is allocated **to the
period in which the launch occurs**, *"regardless of whether the revenue is recognized using the
point-in-time or over-time method"*; the segment revenue line is what was **recognised** in the
period. For over-time HASTE missions the two are different objects: the full transaction price of
six missions lands in the `×6` construction, while the segment line carries only the recognised
portion, *"a significant portion of [HASTE] revenue [having] already been recognized in prior
periods under the overtime accounting method"* ([📄 RKLB Q2 2026 call p.2](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/ect21/2)).
**Launch-period allocation ÷ recognition-period revenue is period-normalisation, and it is
unbounded in sign.**

**`timing` is FALSIFIED, and the control is filed.** Q2 2025 is the zero-HASTE control: *"All five
Electron launch missions completed for the three months ended June 30, 2025 were point-in-time
launches."* Every mission's launch period ≡ its recognition period, so a pure timing wedge must
vanish. It does not:

|---|---|---|---|
| revenue leg | $39,500k | $46,646k | **−15.3%** |
| cost leg | $25,000k | $32,426k | **−22.9%** |

**Both legs diverge at the control. The `timing` hypothesis is dead; period-normalisation is the
