# RKLB × sector-overview — the value-chain position map

**Which position in the stack holds the margin?**

**Answer at RKLB: the position cannot be placed on the map's own basis, and that is the
finding. RKLB files no segment operating margin — only revenue, cost of revenues and gross
profit — and files the reason. On the gross basis its launch segment has just crossed ABOVE
its systems segment, which looks like PIL-2's falsifier firing and is not, because PIL-2's
registered test is stated on OPERATING margin. The test at RKLB is UNEXERCISED, not CLEAN.**

All figures: Rocket Lab Corporation 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-06-30, filed 2026-08-10,
`sec109`. Segment names are the issuer's own (DA-21). `DEMONSTRATED` unless marked otherwise.

## 3. The margin ladder — where RKLB's two rungs sit, and the transfer limit

**Consumed from the 001 universe panorama, not re-derived**: component/enabling suppliers
(TER 32.9%, HEI 25.5%, CW 19.3%, KRMN 19.1%, WWD ~17%) earn roughly **2×** the primes they
supply (LMT 12.4%, RTX 11.4%, LHX 11.1%, NOC 10.1%), and the independent network operator
(GSAT 7.4%) is the worst rung in the monotone ordering.

**RKLB occupies both the best-looking rung and the worst: it is a component supplier into the
merchant market AND a launcher.** The issuer files both: *"Each of these strategic acquisitions
brought incremental vertically-integrated capabilities for our own spacecraft family and also
enabled Rocket Lab to deliver **high-volume manufacturing of critical spacecraft components and
software solutions at scale prices to the broader spacecraft merchant market**"*
([📄 RKLB 10-Q p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/35)).

**⚠️ The ladder does NOT transfer to a segment inside an integrated issuer, and RKLB is the
proof.** If the "component suppliers earn ~2× the primes" rung transferred to RKLB's segment
table, Space Systems (the components segment) should out-earn Launch Services. It does not —
it earns less, on the gross basis, in three of the four periods. **The reasons are structural,
not contradictory:**

1. **The ladder is a whole-company OPERATING margin statement about pure-play component names.
   RKLB's segment table is a gross margin statement about a mixed portfolio.** TER, HEI, CW and
   KRMN carry their own SG&A and R&D; RKLB's segments carry neither.
2. **Space Systems is not a pure component business.** It is *"spacecraft components AND
   spacecraft manufacturing"*, and it now carries seven acquired businesses whose purchase
   accounting runs through cost of revenues. **Acquisitions are named by the issuer as a driver
   of both revenue and cost growth in that segment** ([📄 RKLB 10-Q p.40](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/40)).
3. **The mix differs between the two segments by construction:** Launch Services is **100%
   services** ($0 product revenue, filed); Space Systems is **~96% products**.

**The correct map entry is therefore two rungs with a stated non-comparability**, not one rung
and not a collapsed average. Per the contract's `spread_reported_not_collapsed` rule, **the
spread is the finding**: Launch Services gross margin exceeds Space Systems by **+8.31 pp**
(Q2 2026) and **+8.86 pp** (H1 2026), against **−2.38 pp** and **−6.50 pp** in 2025. **The
spread moved 10.7 pp and 15.4 pp in a year.** Neither endpoint alone is admissible.

## 4. DA-25 / DA-30 — the per-launch pair, on both bases, and the quarantine

RKLB is the only issuer in the universe disclosing both a price and a cost per launch. **Those
two series are also the thesis's most load-bearing numbers, and they are not reproducible from
the audited segment table.** Both bases are reported here, per the `no_single_basis_collapse`
rule.

**Basis 1 — as disclosed** (issuer's own definitions, verbatim): *"Revenue per launch represents
the average transaction price attributable to launch contract performance obligations during
the period in which the launch occurs, regardless of whether the revenue is recognized using the
point-in-time or over-time method… Cost per launch is calculated by taking actual costs of the
launch vehicles that occur in the period, regardless of whether the costs were recognized using
the point-in-time or over-time method **and all period costs in the period of launch**."*
([📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37).)

**Basis 2 — derived from the audited segment table** (Launch Services revenue and cost of
revenues ÷ Electron launch missions completed, both filed).

the threshold they would be tested against.**

**Disposition: the ×N construction is QUARANTINED.** Three consequences, and the third is the
one that matters:

1. **The `timing` hypothesis is inherited as FALSIFIED.** Q2 2025 is the zero-HASTE control —
   all five missions were point-in-time — and the gap still diverges −15.32% / −22.90%. The
   mechanism is **period-normalisation**, not the mission mix, and it is consistent with the
   metric's own definition: it is a *period* figure (*"all period costs in the period of
   launch"*), and the ×N construction treats it as a *per-mission* one.
2. **On the four periods in this filing, three of four breach PIL-5's 5% tolerance on the
   revenue leg and two of four on the cost leg.** This is consistent with 002's
   across-the-longer-series finding (4 of 6 periods). **The metric's own noise is ±20%-class —
   larger than the threshold it is tested against.**
3. **Therefore PIL-5's falsifier and PIL-6's pass-through arm cannot be tested at quarterly
   frequency, and PIL-5's registered `wrong_if`
   (`abs_pct_gap_between_disclosed_cost_per_launch_times_missions_and_audited_launch_segment_cost_of_revenue`,
   `threshold=0.05`) is recorded as UNEXERCISED — not PASS, and not FAIL.** A test whose only
   construction is quarantined on a failed stability test has not been passed; recording it as
   a clearance would be the false clearance F16 forbids. **Evaluate at H1 or FY only**, and only
   with the stability test reported alongside.

**What survives the quarantine, and it is the important part.** The **direction** of the
pass-through finding in §0 item 4 does not depend on the ×N construction at all — it is a
comparison of two disclosed series against each other on the same basis, in the same period, on
identical definitions. **Price rose 15.19% while cost fell 12.00% (Q2).** That is
`DEMONSTRATED` on the issuer's own two filed series. **The LEVELS are not reproducible; the
DIRECTION is robust.** State it that way or not at all.

**And one contamination inside the launch revenue line itself.** Part of the launch revenue
*increase* is not launch revenue: *"increased other launch revenue of $5.7 million, which
includes **contract termination and study revenue**"* (Q2) and *"an increase in other launch
revenue of $7.3 million, which includes contract termination and study revenue and revenue
recognized on over-time HASTE launch missions"* (H1)
([📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37),
[p.40](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/40), [p.42](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/42)).
**A contract termination payment is a settlement, not a launch, and it is inside the series from
which the price trend is read.** Q2's launch revenue fell $2.1M *despite* the $5.7M — i.e.
excluding the settlement the underlying line fell **~$7.8M** on one more mission than the prior
year. `DERIVED`, and it makes the Q2 revenue-per-launch increase harder, not easier, to read as
a price signal.

**Two opposite-signed rates on one activity, again, and both are the filing's:**
launch revenue **+31.63% (H1)** on 12 missions against 10, and **−4.42% (Q2)** on 6 missions
against 5. The issuer's own explanation is *"revenue recognition timing"* plus the two HASTE
missions (revenue recognized over time and partly in earlier quarters). **Quote either only
with its basis** (DA-08, DA-30).

## 8. Carry-forwards

1. **RKLB's launch segment gross margin has crossed above its systems segment (42.86% vs 34.55%
   Q2 2026; 43.73% vs 34.87% H1 2026), and that crossover does NOT fire PIL-2's falsifier.**
   The registered test is on **operating** margin; **RKLB discloses none**, and files why.
   **The test is UNEXERCISED at RKLB — not CLEAN, not FIRED. Manage this as a registered
   false-positive trap.**
2. **The crossover is a services-margin story.** Services GM +9.5 pp (Q2) / +15.1 pp (H1)
   against products +1.8 pp / +2.3 pp. RKLB's launch segment is 100% services; its systems
   segment is ~96% products.
3. **PIL-6's pass-through arm is DEMONSTRATED at RKLB and points the opposite way**: price
   +15.19% while cost −12.00% (Q2); the per-launch spread went **$2.9M → $4.7M**. **The
   direction is robust because it compares two same-basis filed series; the levels are not.**
4. **The ×N per-launch construction is QUARANTINED on a failed stability test** (revenue leg
   range 37.8 pp with a sign change). **PIL-5's wrong_if is `NON-FORMABLE`/UNEXERCISED at
   quarterly frequency — never PASS.** Three of four periods breach the 5% tolerance on the
   revenue leg, two of four on the cost leg, consistent with 002's 4-of-6.
5. **`operator_class: independent_launch` is the positive control, and its demand is 42% one
   government customer.** Three issuers on this map own demand three different ways — captive
   internal (SPCX, ~74%), priced single-customer (RKLB, 42%), subscriber base (the operators) —
   and only RKLB's has a market price in it.
6. **`deal_security_basis: standalone_pre_merger` is load-bearing, not a formality**: RKLB is
   buying IRDM (a universe member, weight 4) for ~$8.0B EV, closing 2027. **IRDM's margin is
   omitted from the ladder because 002 withdrew its DA-23 clearance (F8).** The launcher is
   buying the demand; that is a direction of travel, not a result.
7. **The component identity closes 4 of 4, and DA-23 is confirmed at this issuer across all four
   periods** — `OperatingIncomeLoss` served sign-stripped in every one. **A reader of the
   extracted layer would rank RKLB profitable.** DA-27 is vacuous here; that is not clean.
8. **The 001 ladder does NOT transfer to a segment inside an integrated issuer.** RKLB is the
   proof: its components-and-manufacturing segment earns *less* gross margin than its launch
   segment, because the ladder compares whole-company operating margins of pure-plays and this
   is a within-issuer gross margin over mixed product/service portfolios. **Report the spread
