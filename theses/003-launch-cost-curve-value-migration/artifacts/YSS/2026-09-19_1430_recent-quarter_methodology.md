### 2.2 The component identity, with the opex definition stated

**The opex definition I use at YSS is the EXCLUSIVE one.** YSS files a `Gross profit` subtotal
(`116,343 − 94,193 = 22,150`), so `Total operating expenses` runs from selling, general and
administrative through transaction costs and **excludes cost of revenues**. The identity is
`gross profit − opex = operating income`:

```
Q1 2026   22,150 − 132,616 = (110,466)   ✓ EXACT
Q1 2025   24,602 −  31,233 =  (6,631)   ✓ EXACT
```

Opex build checks independently: `36,706 + 84,696 + 5,289 + 5,925 = 132,616` ✓, and
`26,801 + 0 + 4,401 + 31 = 31,233` ✓.

**The inclusive pairing fails here**, exactly as at PL: `us-gaap:CostsAndExpenses` covers cost
of sales, so pairing the filed gross profit against it double-counts cost of revenues and gives
`22,150 − (94,193 + 132,616) = (204,659)` against a filed `(110,466)` — **1.85× the filed
loss.** Recorded as the failure mode. `EPS × shares` is not used as a sign test anywhere in
this artifact, and at YSS its inadmissibility is demonstrable rather than merely asserted
(§2.4).

## 3. Mode `margin-analysis` — the period traps

### 3.1 DA-27 CANNOT MANIFEST at YSS

YSS is a **31-December filer**, so its fiscal periods and calendar quarters coincide by
construction. **YSS is EXCLUDED from the DA-27 denominator — not counted as clean.** The same
exclusion applies to LUNR. **PL is the only issuer in this trio where DA-27 can manifest, and
it does.**

### 3.2 DA-26 at YSS is UNEXERCISED — an ingestion failure, not a clean result

The FY2025 10-K is present and readable as page text. But the period selector for the fourth
quarter returns nothing:

```
search_xbrl_facts(YSS, fiscal_period="Q4")  →  0 facts
```

**The test did not run.** Per the standing rule, `UNEXERCISED` and `CLEAN` are different
verdicts, and YSS belongs in the first. `UNRESOLVABLE-FROM-PLATFORM`: the remedy is ingestion,
not issuer disclosure — the duration-dated facts exist in the filing, so this is the platform's
gap and not the company's.

### 3.3 The 95% is an IPO-event margin, and I do not quote the adjusted number as a margin

The swing from `(6,631)` to `(110,466)` — a **15.7×** increase in the operating loss on a
**+9.5%** revenue increase — is dominated by two lines that did not exist in the prior-year
period:

| Line | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | Share of the Q1 2026 operating loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock-based compensation expense | 84,696 | — | **76.7%** |
| Transaction costs | 5,925 | 31 | 5.4% |
| **Combined** | **90,621** | 31 | **82.0%** |

**Grade discipline.** `110,466 − 90,621 = 19,845`, or **−17.1%** of revenue. That figure is
**`DERIVED`, not filed** — it is a subtraction performed on filed cells and it is **not
admissible as YSS's margin**, because the costs it removes are real GAAP operating expenses
that the company actually incurred. **A `MODELED` or `DERIVED` input can never satisfy a
falsifier**, so no pillar may rest on −17.1%. It is recorded here to attribute the loss, not to
restate the margin. **The filed margin is `(95)%` and it is the one that counts.**

### 3.4 Gross margin moves in OPPOSITE directions by period basis — a trap I flag on my own work

[📄 YSS 10-Q p.7](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec12/7)), with the issuer's own rounded

**This is a period-basis trap of exactly the kind the brief corrects at SPCX, and an earlier

to PL. **On the Q2 and H1 bases YSS's gross margin rises substantially.** The Q1 decline is

### 3.5 The variable-cost margin is unambiguous, and it rises on all three bases

**The non-GAAP contribution margin is the sharper measure because it is defined on the variable
cost line, and it moves one way on every basis:**

| Period basis | 2026 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (3M) | 34% | 33% | +1 pt |
| Q2 (3M) | **42%** | 24% | **+18 pts** |
| H1 (6M) | **38%** | 29% | **+9 pts** |

Contribution margin is defined as **revenue less direct material costs** — the variable portion
of cost of revenues
([📄 YSS 10-Q p.39](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec9/39),
[📄 YSS 10-Q p.45](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec12/45)). So the direct-material cost per revenue
dollar **fell from `$0.76` to `$0.58`** in the June quarter:

```
Q2 2025   63,555 / 83,839 = $0.758   →  CM% = 24.2%   ✓ matches the published 24%
Q2 2026   53,240 / 92,547 = $0.575   →  CM% = 42.5%   ✓ matches the published 42%
H1 2026  129,517 / 208,890 = $0.620  →  CM% = 38.0%   ✓ matches the published 38%
```

**This is the pass-through the thesis is testing for, and at YSS it is PRESENT and large.** The
reconciliation closes on two independent routes, which is why I accept it: the CM stack up from
gross profit and the direct-material subtraction down from revenue agree **exactly** at both
periods.

```
Q1 2026   22,150 + 10,155 + 3,343 + 4,418 = 40,066   and   116,343 − 76,277 = 40,066   ✓
Q1 2025   24,602 +  7,724 + 1,715 + 1,262 = 35,303   and   106,252 − 70,949 = 35,303   ✓
```

**So the trio splits, and the split is the thesis's finding rather than a refutation of it.**
PL's gross margin falls (57.2% → 56.1% → 53.5%) while its revenue grows **+42.1%** — the
released value is absorbed. **LUNR files no gross-profit subtotal at all**, so it is not
comparable on this axis and a trio census must say so rather than infer a margin for it. **YSS's
variable-cost margin rises sharply on every basis — from `$0.76` to `$0.58` of direct material
per revenue dollar.** A census reporting all three as "losing margin" would be reading one
quarter of one issuer. **On the corrected reading, YSS is a positive case for pass-through, not
a negative one — and it is the only one in this trio.**

### 3.6 The revenue series is not organic, and the sequential decline overstates the weakness

**The revenue series ties exactly across both filings**, which is what makes it usable at all:

## 5. DA-25 — the per-unit figures

**First, the structural premise, because it bounds what the issuer could disclose.** YSS
**"operates in one operating segment and one reportable segment"** — `space infrastructure`,
comprising all of its operations, with the CODM reviewing financial information **on a
consolidated basis** and evaluating return on **total assets**
([📄 YSS 10-Q p.30](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec9/30),
[📄 YSS 10-Q p.34](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec12/34)). **A single-segment issuer can offer no
segment-level margin, no segment-level unit cost, and no segment-level capital allocation.** The
"significant expenses" table is the only disaggregation that exists, and it is an income-statement
walk rather than a unit-economics table. **That is why DA-25 bites at YSS: there is no audited
lower level for a normalised per-unit number to be reproducible *from*.**

### 5.1 The $5,070K per spacecraft is denominator-selected between two co-dated counts

YSS publishes the numerator and both denominators, but **never the ratio**:

| Item | Value | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backlog | `$542,557K` | 2025-12-31 | [📄 YSS 10-K p.68](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec8/68) |
| Spacecraft in backlog | `107` | 2025-12-31 | [📄 YSS 10-K p.5](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec8/5) |
| Spacecraft in orbit | `33` | 2025-12-31 | [📄 YSS 10-K p.68](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec8/68) |

```
542,557 / 107 = 5,070.6   → $5,070K per spacecraft   (backlog denominator)
542,557 /  33 = 16,441.1  → $16,441K per spacecraft  (in-orbit denominator)
16,441 / 5,070 = 3.24×
```

**Same numerator, same date, two denominators published on the same page, and a 3.24× spread
between the answers.** No filed page states either ratio. The figure is therefore
**denominator-selected**: a reader who wants a low number takes 107, one who wants a high number
takes 33, and nothing in the filings adjudicates. **This is DA-25 in its purest form — a
per-unit metric that is *computable* from audited tables but not *determined* by them.**

**Co-dating is the only thing that makes it computable at all.** The prior-year backlog
(`$861,677K` at 2024-12-31) has **no co-dated spacecraft count on the same page**, so there is
**no prior-year ratio and therefore no time series.** `DA-25 CONFIRMED`: **one computable
date, no series, and a 3.24× denominator choice.** Grade `DERIVED`; the ratio is not filed and
must not be quoted as a filed figure.

**And the one computable date cannot be extended forward.** The numerator has moved — backlog
is reported at approximately **`$592 million`** on the Q2 2026 call
([📄 YSS 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec12/37)) against `$542,557K` at 2025-12-31 — but
**no spacecraft count is published anywhere in the Q2 filing.** So the ratio exists at exactly
one date, and at the next date the issuer has published **one half of it**. A reader who
carried `$5,070K` forward as a stable unit cost would be treating a single-point ratio as a
series, which is the specific over-read DA-25 names.

### 5.2 The $21K break between the non-GAAP contribution margin and the segment table

The non-GAAP contribution-margin reconciliation
([📄 YSS 10-Q p.39](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec9/39)) takes a **direct-material** input that
does not tie to the segment table's direct-material line

|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 (3M) | **76,298** | **76,277** | **$21K** |
| Q1 2025 (3M) | 70,950 | 70,949 | $1K |
| H1 2026 | **129,538** | **129,517** | **$21K** |

**A `$21K` break at both the quarter and the half-year, on an input that feeds a published
non-GAAP margin.** The contribution-margin table's own inputs otherwise tie **exactly** through
the stack to the filed cost of revenues:

```
70,949 + 7,724 + 1,715 + 1,262 = 81,650   = the filed Q1 2025 cost of revenues   ✓
```

([📄 YSS 10-Q p.7](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec9/7))

**So the reconciliation closes on one end and is `$21K` short on the other.** The break is
small, and that is precisely why it matters: it is not visible in the published margin, it does
not fail any tie-out a reader would run, and it means the non-GAAP input and the GAAP table are
**not the same quantity**. **DA-25 CONFIRMED as a second instance** — the normalised metric is
not reproducible from the audited table to the dollar, and the unbridged term is
`$21K` of direct material.

**Where the `$21K` sits makes the finding sharper than "two numbers do not tie".** It lands
entirely in **one line — and both tables define that line in the same words.** The segment
footnote defines `Other segment items` as *"other costs of revenue excluding direct materials,
including direct labor, overhead costs and depreciation and amortization"*
([📄 YSS 10-Q p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/YSS/sec12/35)) — naming **exactly** the three
add-backs in the contribution-margin reconciliation. Same line, same definition, `$21K` apart:

| | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 |

| Cost of revenues | 94,193 | 81,650 |
| less direct material, CM basis | (76,277) | (70,949) |
| **= residual, CM route** | **17,916** | **10,701** |

whose definition both tables share is the signature of a measurement difference, not a
presentation one** — which is the distinction that makes this a DA-25 instance rather than a
reconciliation error someone would have caught.

**And it is sub-rounding: the break does not move any published figure.** On the segment basis
the H1 2026 contribution margin is `208,890 − 129,538 = 79,352`, or **37.99%**, against
**38.00%** on the published `129,517` basis. Both print as **38%**. **A defect that changes no
