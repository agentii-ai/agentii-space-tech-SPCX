# SPCX — Segment Boundaries and the DA-21 Restatement

## Finding

**SPCX's "Space" segment is not a launch business. It is a customer business.** The filed
boundary statement is the customer boundary, stated verbatim twice in the same filing:
*"Our Space segment revenue only reflects our customer launches and customer activities."*
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35),
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/36) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`

The consequence is arithmetic, not interpretation. In Q2 2026 SPCX flew **38 launches**
(37 Falcon + 1 Starship), of which **10 were customer launches (26.3%)** and **28 were
internal (73.7%)** [📄 SPCX 10-Q p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35) `[FACT]` ·
`DEMONSTRATED`. **Roughly three quarters of SPCX's launch activity produces no Space segment
revenue by design**, because for internal Starlink launches *"the Company does not recognize
any inter-segment revenue, rather those launch costs are capitalized in satellites in
Property, plant, and equipment, net"* [📄 SPCX 10-Q p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/36)
`[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`. This is DA-06's defining case: for the majority of its launches a
vertically integrated operator has **no transaction price at all**, so no price and no
per-unit cost can be observed for them from the outside.

**And the segment boundary is drawn inside a legal perimeter that itself moved mid-series.**
DA-21 governs the first. It is silent on the second, which is a LIMIT on DA-21 rather than an
application of it — stated in §5 and required reading before any later segment comparison (P2).

## 1. The segment boundary statement, verbatim

SPCX reports **three** operating and reportable segments. The CODM note is filed as: *"The
Company's Chief Executive Officer, as the Chief Operating Decision Maker ("CODM"), organizes
the Company, manages resource allocations, and measures performance among three operating and
reportable segments: (i) Space, (ii) Connectivity, and (iii) AI"*
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.30](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/30) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`.

The boundary of the Space segment is a **revenue-recognition boundary keyed to the
counterparty**, not to the activity:

| Boundary element | Filed language | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Space revenue scope | *"Our Space segment revenue only reflects our customer launches and customer activities."* | [p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35), [p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/36) |
| Why the KPI outgrows it | *"Mass to orbit and launches generally grow more rapidly than Space segment revenue because these metrics include our internal constellation deployments from which we do not recognize inter-segment revenue."* | [p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35) |
| Inter-segment treatment | *"For launches of our Starlink satellites, the Company does not recognize any inter-segment revenue, rather those launch costs are capitalized in satellites in Property, plant, and equipment, net."* | [p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/36) |
| What counts as a launch | *"We designate a launch as a "customer launch" if an external customer payload constitutes the primary payload ... and the mission parameters ... are designed around the primary payload's requirements. To date, all Starship launches have been classified as internal."* | [p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35) |
| Resource-allocation basis | *"The Company's CODM assesses performance and allocates resources to operating segments based on segment income (loss) from operations"* | [p.30](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/30) |
| Asset/liability basis | *"The Company's CODM does not evaluate operating and reportable segments using asset or liability information."* | [p.30](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/30) |

**Correct segment boundary statement for SPCX.** *The Space segment boundary is the CUSTOMER
boundary — management draws the line at whether an external customer is paying, not at
whether a launch occurred. Approximately 74% of launches fall outside it by design and carry
no recognised inter-segment revenue, so the Space segment is a customer series with no
cost-side counterpart for most of the launch activity it nominally describes.*

**Why this is the load-bearing fact for thesis 003.** Any cross-issuer or cross-segment
comparison that reads SPCX's "Space revenue" as a measure of launch *activity* is reading a
customer-payment series. **Space revenue can fall while launch cadence rises**, and the filing
says so in the metric note. That is not a paradox to be explained; it is the boundary working
as filed.

## 3. `12.3%` is three things at once — every basis reported (DA-30)

The commonly quoted *"12.3% of revenue"* is Space segment revenue ÷ **consolidated** revenue,
and it is **three different numbers depending on the basis chosen**. No single basis is quoted
here alone.

| Basis | Q2 2026 | H1 2026 | Numerator | Denominator |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| **(A) Space segment share** | 12.31% | 12.64% | All Space revenue, incl. Dragon and development contracts | Consolidated revenue, incl. X and xAI |

| **(C) Ex-AI Space share** | **18.31%** | **17.32%** | All Space revenue | Consolidated revenue **less AI segment** ($5,253M / $9,129M) |

Sources: Launch Services revenue `$648M` (Q2 2026) and `$978M` (H1 2026)

consolidated revenue per §2. Basis (C)'s denominator is our subtraction on filed cells.

**Basis (A) is a legitimate segment share and an illegitimate launch share, in three separate

**H1 growth rates**, which are different periods. **Direction survives every basis; level
survives none.**

## 4. The component identity — applied to every `operating_income` read

Every `operating_income` read in this artifact is shown as `gross_profit − opex` in-line, with
the opex definition named. **Opex definition used throughout: `Research and development` +
`Selling, general, and administrative` (+ `Restructuring charges (credits)` and `Impairment`
where present) — EXCLUSIVE of cost of revenue.** The filed `Total costs and expenses` line is
**INCLUSIVE** of `Cost of revenue` and is therefore the wrong operand.

**The trap, demonstrated on the consolidated statement.** For Q2 2026: gross profit
`= 7,814 − 3,495 = 4,319`; opex (exclusive) `= 3,548 R&D + 912 SG&A + 2 restructuring = 4,462`;

7,957 = **(3,638)M**`, **false against the filed $(143)M by exactly the $3,495M cost of
revenue** — an overstatement of the loss by 25×. This is the same failure mode recorded at

## 5. DA-21's LIMIT — the entity boundary moved, and DA-21 does not reach it

**This is the section that must survive into every later comparison (P2).** DA-21 governs a
line drawn by management **inside a fixed legal perimeter**. It does **not** reach the case
where the **comparative periods themselves were drawn on a different perimeter**. That case is
present at SPCX, and it is not a segment-boundary question at all.

The legal perimeter changed three times in twenty months, with a fourth change pending
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.11](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/11),
[p.32](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/32), [p.34](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/34) `[FACT]` ·
`DEMONSTRATED`:

| Event | Date | Filed characterisation |
|---|---|---|
| X Merger (Twitter → xAI) | 2025-03-28 | *"xAI completed its acquisition of X Holdings Corp. ("X") and X.AI Corp., in which X and X.AI Corp. became wholly-owned subsidiaries of xAI"* |
| xAI Merger (xAI → SpaceX) | 2026-02-02 | *"the Company completed its acquisition of X.AI Holdings Corp. ("xAI"), pursuant to which xAI became a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Company"* |
| IPO | June 2026 | 638.9M Class A shares at $135.00; net proceeds $85,675M |
| Cursor Merger (Anysphere, Inc.) | pending | Option exercised June 2026; implied equity value $60B; expected to close Q3 2026 |

Both completed mergers sit under a single filed heading — **"Common Control Mergers"** — and
the filing states *"The Mergers were each effected through a share exchange"*
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.11](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/11) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`. That is the
carryover basis: a share exchange between entities under common control, under which the
comparative columns are **re-presented as if the combining entities had always been combined**.

**(a) The comparatives were RECAST, not merely restated.** Two independently filed items put
X-originated economics inside the Q2 2025 comparative column, in a period before X was a
SpaceX subsidiary:

- The **Q2 2025 comparative contains $426M of Advertising revenue** — X's business — a line
  that did not exist in the SpaceX perimeter at all before the X Merger
  [📄 SPCX 10-Q p.13](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/13) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`.
- The **Q2 2025 comparative AI segment contains $190M of restructuring charges** which the
  filing attributes to *"In 2022, X, an indirect subsidiary of the Company (through the X
  Merger and subsequently, xAI Merger), initiated global employee workforce reductions"* —
  an event that predates SpaceX's ownership of X by three years
  [📄 SPCX 10-Q p.32](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/32) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`. This is
  precise, not inferred: in the Q2 2025 segment table the restructuring row reads
  `Space —, Connectivity —, AI 190, Total 190`, so the charge sits **inside the AI segment's
  Q2 2025 result**, and the $194M H1 2025 charge likewise sits entirely in AI
  [📄 SPCX 10-Q p.31](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/31),
  [p.32](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/32).

**(b) Therefore the headline growth rate is a recast-column growth rate.** Filed growth is
**+91.9% Q2 / +53.7% H1**. The legacy-perimeter control — consolidated **less** the AI segment,
i.e. **Space + Connectivity = the SpaceX that existed before both mergers** — grows
**+57.6% Q2 / +36.8% H1**:

Both growth rates are arithmetic on filed segment cells; the ex-AI row is our subtraction.
**The 34.3 pp / 16.9 pp gap IS the entity boundary's size**, and it is larger than most of the
effects this thesis is trying to measure. Any later artifact that reports a SPCX growth rate
on one basis only violates DA-30 at the largest magnitude in the thesis.

**Consolidated revenue is `Space + Connectivity + AI`, and AI is the acquired piece.** So the
ex-AI row is not an arbitrary cut — it is the **legacy-perimeter** growth rate. But it is a
**definitional control on SPCX's own segment line**: it removes acquired *revenue* from both
numerator and denominator at the segment level. It does **not** restore comparability to any
**as-filed prior period**, because no as-filed prior period exists for this registrant — this
is SPCX's first 10-Q. Any later artifact treating `+57.6%` as "the organic growth rate" is
treating a segment-line subtraction as if it were a restated comparative. It is not. `[VIEW]`

**`+$1,824M` is not evidence of migration.** The AI segment's revenue increase of

## 6. Mode: `business-model-classification`

**Business Model**: **Hybrid — vertically integrated platform plus service, with a small
product component.** `[VIEW]`

**Core Offering**: three offerings under one consolidated entity whose only common factor is
that two of the three are *demand* businesses rather than *supply* businesses `[FACT]` ·
`DEMONSTRATED`:

| Segment | Core offering | Revenue type | Q2 2026 revenue | Share |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Space | Launch Services for commercial and government customers (Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy); Launch and Development for government agency space programmes (Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, Starship, Dragon) | Fixed-price contract | $962M | 12.31% |
| Connectivity | Starlink broadband and mobile, >10,200 satellites, 167 countries/territories | Subscription service | $4,291M | 54.91% |
| AI | Grok frontier model, AI solutions, X platform, AI computational infrastructure | Advertising + subscription + compute | $2,561M | 32.78% |

Revenue by nature [📄 SPCX 10-Q p.13](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/13) `[FACT]` ·
`DEMONSTRATED`: **Services $7,353M / Products $461M** in Q2 2026 (H1: $11,667M / $841M), and
the filing states *"All of products revenue is attributable to the Connectivity segment."* The
"product" component of the business model — customer premises equipment — therefore sits
**entirely inside Starlink**, and the Space segment is a **pure service/contract business**.

**Positioning**: **High-end in Connectivity; low-end in Space.** `[VIEW]` The rationale is
numeric and pulls in opposite directions. Connectivity carries a **51.99% gross margin**
(`(4,291 − 2,060) / 4,291`, Q2 2026) and a **+38.59% operating margin** — the profile of a
premium, price-setting service. Space carries a **65.80% gross margin** on the same SEGMENT
basis (`(962 − 329) / 962`) but a **−56.34% operating margin**: the $633M gross profit is more
than consumed by $1,076M of R&D against $962M of revenue. Space's cost structure is dominated
by **development spend, not delivery cost** — which is what a captive integrator looks like,
not a merchant launcher. Gross margin here must be quoted on the segment basis; the
**consolidated** gross margin is **55.27%** (`4,319 / 7,814`) and the two are not
interchangeable (DA-30).

**Structural note — the Space segment is mis-described by its own name.** A business whose
revenue is earned only on customer launches, whose costs include internal-constellation
deployments that generate no revenue, and which allocates a *"significant amount of launch
capacity"* to its own Connectivity segment — is a **captive integrator**, not an independent
launcher: `operator_class = captive_integrated` in the value-pool map's enum. `[DEDUCTED]` The
evidence is the asymmetry between the filed KPIs. Q2 2026 mass to orbit was **485 t** (customer
payload **87 t**, internal **397 t**); Q2 2025 was **652 t** (customer 88 t, internal 563 t)
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`. **Internal
payloads are 81.9% of Q2 2026 mass to orbit (397 of 485) and contribute $0 of Space revenue.**
Total mass to orbit fell **−25.6% YoY** while *customer* payload mass fell only **−1.1%**; the
entire decline is internal. A launch-cost-curve thesis that reads SPCX's launch output off any
total-activity metric will be reading a number whose movement is driven by Starlink deployment
cadence — and the capitalized cost of that activity sits in Satellites within PP&E, a filed
**$13,788M** net-of-depreciation-balance component at June 30, 2026
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.14](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/14) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`. **The launch
cost of the internal programme is not on the income statement at all.** It is on the balance
sheet, and it will return as depreciation — $2,735M in Q2 2026 against $1,310M in Q2 2025.

**Non-comparability, stated in-line.** Because SPCX is `captive_integrated` and a merchant
launcher such as RKLB is `enabler`, **SPCX's Space segment margin of −56.34% (Q2 2026) is not
comparable to RKLB's Launch Services margin of 42.86%, on any margin line, in either
direction.** One number is struck against a market price for every mission; the other is struck
against revenue from 26.3% of missions while carrying the cost of the other 73.7% inside a
capitalised balance-sheet programme. The 99-point gap between them measures the difference
between the two **structures**, not a difference in operating efficiency — and any cross-issuer
benchmark that ranks the two on margin is comparing a price to an accounting residual. Cross-
issuer launch comparison here has to be made on cost per kilogram (PIL-1's basis), and at SPCX
the largest part of that denominator is unfiled.

## 7. Mode: `distribution-channel-analysis`

**Distribution Model**: **Direct sales in all three segments, in three different
configurations. No indirect channel, reseller, or channel-partner programme is disclosed for
any segment.** `[VIEW]`

| Segment | Channel | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Space | Direct to customer (commercial and government), milestone-based contracts | [p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/36) |
| Connectivity | **Two-tier**: self-serve e-commerce plus a managed direct sales force | [p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35) |
| AI | Advertising sales plus direct AI-infrastructure/cloud contracts | [p.13](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/13) |

**Current Channel Mix — and the KPI measures exactly one of the two tiers.** The disclosed
Starlink subscriber KPI is defined as a *"unique service line ... registered to a person or
entity that does not have a direct, negotiated agreement with the Starlink sales team"*, and
expressly *"does not include managed enterprise and government customers with contracts in
domains including aviation, maritime, land mobility, fixed sites, and government entities"*
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.35](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/35),
[p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/36) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`. The managed channel is
therefore **inside Connectivity's $4,291M revenue and outside its 12.0M subscriber count**.
`[DEDUCTED]` Any per-subscriber metric computed as Connectivity revenue ÷ 12.0M is
contaminated by an omitted channel and is not a valid unit-economics input (DA-09, DA-10).

The two disclosed channel metrics point in opposite directions, which is the point
[📄 SPCX 10-Q p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/SPCX/sec8/36) `[FACT]` · `DEMONSTRATED`:

| Metric | June 30, 2026 | June 30, 2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Starlink subscribers (self-serve only) | 12.0M | 6.0M | **+100.0%** |
| Starlink ARPU | $66 / month | $85 / month | **−22.4%** |

Self-serve subscriber count doubled while blended self-serve ARPU fell by nearly a quarter.
That is exactly DA-10's reading — *"a mix-shift artifact of adding lower-priced plans rather
than price erosion"* — and the filing supports the mix-shift reading mechanically: the
definition counts a household's residential line and its roam line as **two** subscribers.
`[DEDUCTED]`

**Historical Channel Mix Trend (Trailing 3 Years)**: **`UNEXERCISED`, not clean.** This is
SPCX's first 10-Q as a registrant; no 3-year channel series exists in the corpus, and the
skill's default `temporal_scope` (4 trailing quarters, max 8) cannot be met on any channel
measure. Worse, any "prior" mix would be measured on a **different legal perimeter** (§5). An
unengaged check is not a passed check; this is recorded as a coverage limit with the class
below.

**Strategic Implication**: **the channel with the most pricing power is the one that is not
counted.** `[VIEW]` Negotiated pricing lives in the managed channel (aviation, maritime,
government, fixed sites) and that channel is disclosed as revenue but **not** as subscribers.
The self-serve channel is where the disclosed ARPU decline lives. A read concluding "Starlink
ARPU is eroding" is observing the self-serve channel only, and cannot separate channel mix-shift
from price erosion. The disclosure that would resolve it — subscribers and ARPU for the managed
channel — is not filed, and SPCX's own risk-factor and KPI sections do not supply it.
