## Key arguments

**A1 — The cost curve's only measured point moves the *wrong way*, and that is the thesis.**
Electron is the single vehicle in the universe with a `DEMONSTRATED` marginal cost. Measured
correctly — excluding two of six Q2 2026 missions that were suborbital testbeds carrying **zero
kg to LEO** — cost per launch **fell 12.0%** ($5.0M → $4.4M) while cost per **kilogram rose
32.0%** ($16,667 → $22,000/kg). **This means** the sector's headline productivity claim is, at
its best-measured point, a denominator artefact in the optimistic direction — so any thesis
pricing launch-cost decline into a revenue model is pricing a number that has not appeared.
`0.88 × 1.50 = 1.32` exactly.

**A2 — Launch-cost ambiguity is a *denominator* problem, not a measurement problem, and it is
five times larger on that axis.** SPCX's 8-K **prints all four numerators and both denominators
on one page**; the four `$/kg` values that follow from them are **arithmetic, not disclosure** —
they appear nowhere in the exhibit, and every one of the four is correct. The spread is
**12.8×**, and the decomposition is exact: numerator **1.617×**, denominator **7.886×**.
**Therefore** the report must name a basis on every rung, because the spread will otherwise be
read as *uncertainty* when it is actually *definition* — and a reader who treats it as noise
will average values that are not commensurable. **The derivation is the point**: a reader can
reproduce all four from the issuer's own page, which is exactly why none of them can be
dismissed as an error.

**A3 — The value pool went to whoever owns the demand, and the market has now said so twice, in
cash.** Both independent operators in the universe were acquired within twelve months: **GSAT by
Amazon at $90.00/share** and **IRDM by RKLB at $54.00/share**. Amazon pays ≈**41.4×** TTM
revenue for a **−7.37%** operating margin; RKLB pays ≈**8.3×** for a **+15.10%** one — a ≈5.0×
multiple ratio *against* a 22.5-point margin differential in the opposite direction. **This
argues for** treating operator margin as non-informative about strategic value, and for reading
a constellation's worth as its demand relationship rather than its income statement. Anchor (a)
holds — component suppliers earn **2.02×** the primes they supply — but anchor (b) is
**falsified**: the worst margin is **FLY's −80.90%, a manufacturer**, not the single-customer
operator.

**A4 — The data-integrity register is now the finding, not the caveat — and it caught this
report's own source.** DA-23 (the platform serves a filed negative as a positive of identical
magnitude) is confirmed at **9 of 9** universe tickers. The sharpest instance is not an issuer
but an artifact: **the GSAT analysis written to document DA-23 was itself reproducing it on
eight figures**, including a claimed divergence that existed only because the artifact had read
a stripped value — and **all three automated gates passed on the stripped version**. **This
means** a clean gate result is not evidence of a clean number, and every figure a downstream
thesis inherits should carry the basis it was read on.

**A5 — `NON-FORMABLE` is a result, and refusing to print a number is the finding.** PIL-6's
falsifier needs launch cost as a share of *programme* cost. At **three of four** demand-side
names the quantity **cannot be drawn at all** — YSS's cost of revenues decomposes exhaustively
into four components that exclude launch (a **presence** finding, not an absence); PL names
launch and quantifies it nowhere; SATS has named agreements with no dollar amount on either
side. The one above-bar value, **LUNR's 14.19%**, is a consolidated denominator that collapses
to 2.80–4.37% as a **denominator event**, not a pass-through. **This implies** the demand side
does not price off the curve — and the honest form of that claim is "unmeasurable at three of
four names," not "zero."

## Story line

**The report opens on a single measurement and closes on the reason it cannot be generalised.**
The sector's cost-curve story rests on one vehicle that discloses its cost per launch; measured
against the right denominator, that one point goes the wrong way. The report then widens: the
same ambiguity that hides the inversion (**denominator, not measurement**) is five times larger
than the basis question everyone argues about, and it is *structural* — 13 of 20 matrix cells
are absent, and six of those absences sit in the class that **more research cannot fix** because
the contract has no admissible field for them.

**The middle turn is the money.** If the launcher is not where the value is, where is it? The
answer is now priced: two acquirers, twelve months apart, bought the *customer relationship*
underneath two operators — one of them loss-making — and neither paid for the margin. The
margin ladder says the same thing structurally: it is **bimodal**, high at the demand-owning end
and negative through the middle, and **SPCX's launch segment holds both ends simultaneously** —
first of three on gross margin, last of three on operating.

**The report closes on discipline rather than on a conclusion.** The register that classifies
these defects was itself defeated by one of them, inside an artifact written to document it —
and no automated check saw it. Six pillars are reported with their falsifier outcomes, five
corrections this thesis made to its predecessors, and four disposition classes kept distinct
because their remedies differ: a research queue, a tooling queue, a schema-change queue, and a
number that cannot be drawn. **The recommendation is not a trade** — this thesis produces no
positions by design — **but a citation rule**: carry the basis with the grade, or inherit a
figure that reproduces and means something else.
