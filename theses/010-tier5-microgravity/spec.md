## 0. Inherited baseline (to be completed on activation)

001's Pillar 4 covered microgravity through UTHR, and its conclusion is the most
**negative-but-honest** in the workspace. Inherited, not to be re-derived:

- **F3's physics is `DEMONSTRATED`; its economics are not.** Microgravity genuinely
  improves protein crystal growth (no sedimentation), ZBLAN fluoride fiber (suppresses
  crystallization), and small-molecule polymorph control (the ritonavir Form III result,
  published in *Nature*).
- **The correct test is value-per-kg-returned versus fully-loaded cost-per-kg-returned** —
  launch, on-orbit processing, reentry capsule and recovery combined. **Not TAM.**
- **Both halves of that test are unavailable.** Value-per-kg is not disclosed (Varda has
  no revenue). Cost-per-kg is not disclosed (Varda is private; the ~$329M raised and
  ~50 kg/mission figures are **`CLAIMED` press figures only**). 001's verdict: **"the
  clearest `UNRESOLVABLE-FROM-PUBLIC-SOURCES` case in the thesis"** — a *stronger*
  condition than PIL-6 (public but unreachable) or P5 (commercially licensed), because
  **these figures do not exist publicly at all.**
- **PIL-4 nonetheless HOLDS** on a *measured* zero: no commercial-scale microgravity
  manufacturing commitment from UTHR, MRK, BMY or AMGN.
- **UTHR runs an 87.3% gross margin and a 42.2% operating margin** on $783.3M quarterly
  revenue. **87.3% is the bar any microgravity process must clear.** UTHR revenue was
  **−1.9% YoY**, so microgravity is **optionality, not a response to pressure**.
- **The "immaterial-to-the-counterparty" pattern** reaches here: UTHR is large enough
  that its Varda partnership (announced May 2026, first samples "as early as 2027") is
  financially irrelevant, so it is never broken out — **which suppresses the very
  evidence the falsifier needs to fire.**
- **P6: Varda enters as a value-chain node only, with UTHR as its named listed proxy.**
  No private company receives its own thesis.

## 1. Research Question

**Given that the economic test cannot be settled from public sources, is the correct
posture to (a) hold microgravity as unpriced optionality within UTHR, (b) treat it as
uninvestable until a listed pure-play or a disclosure exists, or (c) monitor for the
specific events that would make it investable — and what are those events, precisely?**

The thesis's honest output may be a **watch-list with named triggers** rather than a
position. That is a legitimate result: the constitution permits a pillar to be recorded
`UNRESOLVABLE-FROM-PUBLIC-SOURCES` rather than dropped or failed.

## 3. Skill Deployment Matrix (outline — not frozen)

1. **The economic test, framed rather than settled.** Report the value-per-kg and
   cost-per-kg *ranges* implied by the `CLAIMED` figures, and state plainly that both
   halves are `CLAIMED`. A falsifier that cannot fire is not evidence.
2. **The 87.3% gross-margin bar.** Microgravity must beat a process the incumbent
   already runs profitably at that margin. Quantify what value uplift per kg would be
   required.
3. **Named triggers that would make this investable** — the specific disclosures:
   a listed pure-play (Varda or a competitor listing), UTHR breaking out programme
   economics, a second named pharma partner, or a commercial-scale (not pilot) volume
   commitment. Each with expected timing and source.
4. **Why the demand side is silent**: revenue −1.9% YoY at UTHR means optionality, not
   urgency. Is there any listed pharma where microgravity addresses a *pipeline
   problem* rather than being a research curiosity?
