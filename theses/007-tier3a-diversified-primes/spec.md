## 0. Inherited baseline (to be completed on activation)

001 covered the primes only at **Light/Standard supply-chain depth** — they appear in
`_cross/phase-3-production-supply.md` and in the `supply-chain` rows of the 001 matrix,
but no prime received a standalone business analysis. The constitution is explicit that
space is a **minority of revenue** at BA/LMT/NOC/LHX/RTX and that they are to be used
for *low-beta space exposure and supply-chain read-through, never as a space thesis in
itself*.

**The inheritance is therefore negative and must be stated as such**: 001 established
that these names are not space pure-plays. 007 does not re-derive that. It asks the
different question below.

## 1. Research Question

**For a book that cannot get space exposure at size from the pure-plays, do the primes
offer a better risk-adjusted route to the same secular driver — or does the space
segment's growth get diluted below the threshold where it moves the consolidated
multiple?**

## 3. Skill Deployment Matrix (outline — not frozen)

1. **Space-segment contribution to consolidated revenue and operating income**, stated
   per issuer on that issuer's own segment definition — **DA-21 applies**: LMT's Space ≠
   NOC's Space ≠ LHX's Space, segment boundaries are drawn by management and change over
   time, and naive aggregation across primes double-counts and omits.
2. **Segment-level EBITDA and margin** is the crux: a space arm worth a high multiple
   inside a consolidated entity trading at a defense multiple may be **sum-of-the-parts
   invisible**. Test whether an SOTP reveals value the market ignores.
3. **Fixed-price development programme risk** — the primes' characteristic failure mode
   (charges on cost-type-vs-fixed-price transition), and whether any space programme
   carries it.
4. **The ULA and Aerojet read-throughs**: propulsion is a duopoly-adjacent input to
   launch. Does it carry pricing power or is it a cost centre?

**Candidate binding constraint — `DEMAND`.** Justification to be tested on activation:
the primes' space revenue is government-programme-driven, and the binding question is
whether budget and award flow sustains it — not whether they can build it.
