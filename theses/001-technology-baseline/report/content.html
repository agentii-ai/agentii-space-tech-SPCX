Falsifier Evaluation · Lines 3 and 4

Lines 3–4 — fixed-cost absorption and the microgravity hurdle

Line 3 — Manufacturing rate binds, not launch capacity

metric=share_of_named_issuers_citing_launch_availability_as_primary_delay_cause threshold=0.5 op=>

The bound. Not a cost but a ratio: revenue must absorb a fixed operating base at the observed gross margin. Planet Labs earned a 53.5% gross margin — the best of any space operator here — and still lost 37.1% at the operating line, because opex was 90.6% of revenue. Break-even is 1.69× current revenue. At YSS's 24.0% gross margin the same arithmetic requires 2.86×.

Falsifier result: unresolvable, and the reason matters. The risk-factor language is filed and public. What does not exist is any structured field for delay cause. The census is unbuilt, not unavailable — bounded work across 19 filings, and the only one of the three with a remedy inside the platform.

Verdict: indeterminate — and the adjacent finding is stronger than the falsifier. The measured constraint is fixed-cost absorption, visible in XBRL without reading a word of risk-factor prose. Recommendation: re-scope this falsifier to the break-even multiple, which is computable and comparable across issuers.

The YSS detail, at line level. Revenue $92.547M, cost of revenue $70.367M, gross profit $22.180M at 24.0%; operating expenses $63.493M, or 68.6% of revenue; operating result $(41.313)M; R&D $5.766M, or 6.2%. The fixed cost base is 2.9× the gross profit — which is the whole story. A 24% gross margin is a real, positive manufacturing spread; the company is below minimum efficient scale, and manufacturing cost is not the binding constraint — manufacturing volume is. Those are different claims with different remedies. Caveat: Q2 2026 includes the All.Space acquisition, so the expense ratio may be inflated by deal costs; segment confirmation was not available from the extract.

Line 4 — Demonstrated physics, unproven economics

metric=listed_pharma_microgravity_commercial_manufacturing_disclosure threshold=0 op=>

The bound. The hurdle is the incumbent process's return. Amgen: $10,054M revenue less $2,811M COGS gives 72.0% gross. Bristol Myers: 71.3%. Two independent buyers agree to within 0.7 points — that convergence is the number an orbital process must clear.

Falsifier result. Zero of four listed pharma disclose commercial microgravity manufacturing.

Verdict: supported on the disclosure falsifier, unresolvable on the economic test. Value-per-kg and cost-per-kg for any microgravity product do not exist publicly — a stronger condition than an unreachable registry, because here the number exists nowhere. The line holds on the weaker of its two tests, and the register says so.

Falsifier Evaluation · Lines 5 and 6

Lines 5–6 — orbital compute and allocated spectrum

Line 5 — Does orbital compute close before terrestrial wins?

metric=orbital_to_terrestrial_cost_per_kW_ratio threshold=3 op=< basis=ANY_OF_A_B_C

Falsifier result: unresolvable, on one side only. The denominator is fully public — Microsoft's capex and capacity guidance give a terrestrial cost per kW on all three bases. Line 6 — Finite allocated resources, priced as assets

metric=new_entrant_primary_spectrum_or_slot_grant_without_incumbent_acquisition threshold=0 op=>

The bound. The only recent transaction pricing allocated spectrum is SPCX–EchoStar at $19.6B for AWS-4, H-Block and AWS-3 — carried as a transaction value, not a unit price, because the per-MHz-POP denominator is not disclosed and a bare ratio would fail the contract.

The premise is demonstrated four ways. EchoStar's quarterly operating margin runs 2.3% → 5.7% → 460.5% → 118.1% → 10.7% — two quarters carrying the spectrum gain, the rest the actual operating business at 2–11%. That is roughly $27B of gains across 2025 H2 against $15.0B of full-year revenue: the asset was worth ~1.8× the business holding it. SPCX's 10-Q shows $856M of spectrum payments in cash — a headline price is an assertion, a cash-flow line is a payment. Iridium is profitable at 15.1% on licensed L-band. Grading: the $856M is filed — fact. The $27B is an inference from GAAP operating income whose classification the filing does not break out — the direction is certain, the figure is deducted.

Two caveats, and they limit the premise's reach. First, this is a sell-side observation, not a buy-side one — EchoStar realised the value by exiting spectrum, so it demonstrates the exit, not that holding is durable. Second, the buy-side counterexample cuts: Iridium's operating margin fell 8.1 points while revenue grew 3.8%, because a durable licence does not confer pricing power in the service layer above it.

Verdict: indeterminate. The falsifier needs FCC IBFS and the ITU Space Network List — public, outside the reachable corpus. The premise does not need the falsifier, and the falsifier does not weaken the premise.

Two more signals from the same filing

  • The only new risk factor this quarter is about terrestrial data centers. Item 1A carries exactly one addition, headed "Risks related to our AI infrastructure and data center operations." Not launch. Not Starship. Not spectrum. The market's mental model of SPCX and the company's own risk disclosure have diverged.
  • F1's power bound is confirmed as a live constraint — on the ground. That risk factor names "power constraints" among data-center threats, and the forward-looking list includes "our ability to obtain sufficient power, GPUs." The power constraint is real, binding, and the first company to hit it is hitting it terrestrially.
  • And the thermal bound has already been observed in flight. Starcloud-1 carried an NVIDIA H100 and cannot run it at full power because cooling capacity is insufficient — F2 observed rather than derived, and the first empirical data point in the thesis. Graded CLAIMED: press report, private company.

    • The bound. Radiative rejection follows P/A = ε·σ·T⁴: 413 W/m² at 300 K, or 2,419 m² of radiator per MW, falling to 313 m² at 500 K — a 7.7× compression. The array is temperature-independent and the radiator is not, so total deployed area runs 7,499 m²/MW on solar against 313 m²/MW on nuclear at 500 K. Removing the array entirely produces that 24× reduction — the concrete quantitative case for a space reactor. The array, not the radiator, is the dominant area term, which is why Line 2 reports the full DA-15 temperature range rather than assuming one value. And the terrestrial gap decides this line: 7,499 m² of deployed surface per MW against roughly 200–400 m² of building footprint — 20–35× more, which no cost curve bends. At the F5 propellant floor the ~50 t/MW is $2.3–4.6M per MW in launch cost alone.

The conclusion is not that orbital compute was out-competed. It is that it was out-chosen by the only party able to choose it — with $21.5B of disclosed capital expenditure, and a preference that is terrestrial.

Why the capital does not move. Platform Data Quality · Period and Classification Defects

Period and classification defects

DA-26 and DA-27 — two period defects, one value and one label

DA-26, annual figures mislabelled as quarterly, is universal at 23 of 23 issuers. It is a whole-statement failure: at Amgen the Q4 row carries the annual total for both revenue and operating income, confirmed at TER, CW and VRT — a model keyed on the Q4 row overstates both lines by roughly 4×. It lands on the year-end quarter at non-calendar issuers, the subtlest form. Scope caveat: the rows are reliably not a quarter, but not reliably a full year — WWD's FY2025 row implies a year-end quarter that dips 16% sequentially mid-sequence, which no other issuer's annual reading requires.

DA-27, labels derived from the calendar quarter, is 4 of 4. The platform buckets a period by calendar quarter from 1 January, then labels it with the issuer's fiscal year — exact for December year-ends, off by one otherwise. Four non-calendar issuers, four offsets; every December issuer, none. A pattern that separates a population perfectly on one variable is a mechanism, not a coincidence. The two defects compound at non-calendar issuers.

DA-24 — a second mechanism, with an identical consequence

EchoStar's 2025 Q3 and Q4 operating income is not operating: a spectrum-licence sale was classified such that it flows through the operating line, producing margins of 460% and 118%. Distinct from DA-23 — this defect is classification, not sign — but the consequence is the same, and so is the remedy: verify operating income against its components before use. The single rule covers both mechanisms across three issuers. Any screen ranking EchoStar's operating income is comparing a licence sale against operating businesses.

Five findings the thesis was not looking for

  • Margin runs inverted to narrative. Component suppliers earn roughly 2× the primes that buy from them; the capital-equipment supplier earns 3×; the growth names earn nothing. No issuer here offers both growth and margin except TER and Karman.
  • Fixed-cost absorption binds, confirmed at seven issuers. The best gross margin in the sector still loses 37% at the operating line.
  • Orbital compute was out-chosen, not out-built — by the only actor with cheap orbital access, which deployed 1.4 GW terrestrially and named power constraints in its only new risk factor.
  • The demand end is 73× the supply end and unpersuaded. The barrier is willingness against a 72% incumbent margin, not capacity.
  • Half the critical inputs are structurally unpriced — and the clearest case is named: space-grade solar cells are a two-supplier market, SolAero (RKLB) and Spectrolab (BA), supplying the component whose efficiency is the first term in bound F1, with one leg a subsidiary of a launch competitor. But it is structural, not priced — neither discloses those units separately. The same applies to liquid engines, solid motors and rad-hard electronics: Lockheed reports Space as a named segment and still aggregates the components away, and ULA is equity-method and invisible at both parents.
Resolution Path · What Would Change Each Verdict

The six lines and the disclosure that would move each

The open questions here are disclosure questions, not physics questions — three of the six bounds derive from constants (F1, F2, F5), the rest from filings and one transaction.

Line 1 — Launch cost floor · HOLDS

A customer list price or a fully-loaded cost at commercial cadence. No issuer discloses either. Cheaper launch does not fire this falsifier — price is not cost.

Line 2 — Orbital power envelope · HOLDS

Orbital-compute revenue on any of four readings. SPCX's AI-infrastructure revenue is adjacent and fails the four-way reading — a documented false-positive trap.

Line 3 — Constellation economics · UNRESOLVABLE, unbuilt census

A coding of Item 1A and MD&A delay language across 19 issuers. The language is filed; nothing structures it — the only line with a remedy inside the platform.

Line 4 — Microgravity · HOLDS on the falsifier, UNRESOLVABLE on the economics

A value-per-kg or cost-per-kg figure for any microgravity product. Neither exists — Varda is private; UTHR's partnership is too small to force a segment.

Line 5 — Orbital compute · UNRESOLVABLE, numerator absent

An operator-disclosed orbital cost per kW. The denominator is public; the numerator is disclosed by nobody.

Line 6 — Spectrum and slots · UNRESOLVABLE, corpus gap

A query of FCC IBFS and the ITU Space Network List for primary grants to entities with no prior US holdings. Public, and outside the reachable corpus.
