## Headline: the denominator is filed — and it is still the wrong denominator

001's carry-forward #2 asked for a filed source for Electron's 300 kg. **It exists, twice,
in the FY2025 10-K Item 1 Business.** The claim-vs-filed restatement is **0.00%**, not ±15%.
That carry-forward is discharged.

**The ±15% test nevertheless is NOT MET.** The filed figure is a *capacity ceiling* ("up to
300 kg"), asserted by the issuer about its own vehicle, in a document whose denominator
question — *what mass was actually delivered to LEO, and at which reference orbit* — is
answered nowhere in the filed record. Two channels each move the demonstrated $/kg by
**≥50%**, and the filing closes neither.

| Question | Answer | Δ to demonstrated $/kg |
|---|---|---|
| 001's claim 300 kg → filed 300 kg | **300 kg, filed twice** | **0.00%** — carry-forward discharged |
| Denominator band holding ±15% | **260.9 – 352.9 kg** | — |
| Channel O — reference orbit (SSO ~200 kg) | **unclosed** | **+50.0%**, both bases, period-independent |
| Channel M — HASTE mix in the mission count | **partially filed** | 0% to +50.0% (B) / 0% to −31.3% at the circulating proxy (A) |
| Channel S — non-launch revenue inside the segment line | **filed, magnitude ~13%** | ±13%-class noise on the cross-check |
| **±15% test** | **NOT MET** | falsifier does not fire on the literal test; band is unclosed |

**Direction matters and is favourable to the thesis**: on basis B every open channel pushes
$/kg **up**. The nameplate-ceiling denominator *understates* cost per kilogram. PIL-1's
conclusion is therefore robust to this residual — but its **band** is not ±15%, and those
are two different claims. The ledger must carry both.

## 1. The denominator, filed

Both statements are Item 1 Business, FY2025 10-K, and a `300 kg` keyword sweep of `sec87`
returns **exactly pages 7 and 8** — no third occurrence.

> **p.7** — "Launch Services: We currently provide reliable and responsive launch services
> into low earth orbit on Electron for spacecraft **up to 300 kg**."
> [📄 RKLB 10-K p.7](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/7)

> **p.8** — "It is capable of deploying spacecraft of **up to 300 kg to low Earth orbit**
> across a wide range of orbital inclinations from 38 to 120 degrees from our operational
> LC-1 in Mahia New Zealand and a wide range of orbital inclinations from 38 to 60 degrees
> from our operational LC-2 in Wallops Island, Virginia."
> [📄 RKLB 10-K p.8](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec87/8)

**Locality of the source is itself the finding.** The Q2 2026 10-Q (`sec109`) and Q1 2026
10-Q (`sec104`) contain **no** `300 kg` at all — vehicle specs live in the 10-K's Item 1
Business and nowhere else. A reader searching the most recent quarterly filing for the
denominator finds nothing. 001's `CLAIMED` grade was correct for the document class it was
reading; it was wrong only in inferring that no filed source existed.

**The ±15% band, in denominator units.** $/kg ∝ 1/D identically on both bases, so the band is
basis-independent:

```
  $/kg = X / D      Δ = 300/D − 1
  Δ = +0.15  →  D = 300/1.15 = 260.9 kg
  Δ = −0.15  →  D = 300/0.85 = 352.9 kg
  → the denominator band holding every Electron $/kg inside ±15% is 260.9 – 352.9 kg
```

The filed 300 kg sits at the **centre** of that band. Channel O (below) does not.

**Three structural facts about the filed figure, all from p.8, all load-bearing:**

1. It reads **"up to"** — a maximum over an envelope, not a per-mission figure.
2. The envelope is **38–120° from LC-1** but only **38–60° from LC-2**. Sun-synchronous
   orbit is ~97–98° inclination: it is inside LC-1's range and **outside LC-2's range**.
   Any SSO mission is structurally confined to Mahia, and the filed record does not state
   capacity at 98°.
3. It is a **capacity ceiling**. Delivered mass ≤ 300 kg, always. The denominator can only
   be too high, never too low, for a given mission.

**No XBRL instrument reaches this figure.** `list_xbrl_concepts(search="payload")` returns
**0 rows**; `(search="kg")` likewise. The 300 kg is narrative-only, so no structured query,
recast, or calculation linkbase can validate or restate it. **The denominator of the only
`DEMONSTRATED` per-launch cost in the universe is unqueryable.**

## 2. The ±15% test: three channels

### 2.1 The literal test — PASSES at 0.00%

001's claim and the filed figure are the same number. The registered falsifier
(`abs_pct_change_in_demonstrated_price_per_kg_to_LEO_after_denominator_validation`,
threshold 0.15) **does not fire**: Δ = **0.00%** on both bases.

This is a genuine upgrade — `CLAIMED` → filed (SOX-certified, Section 302/906 certified,
10b-5 exposure) — but it is an upgrade of **evidence class, not of independence**. It is the
issuer's own assertion about its own vehicle. Under spec §1b, a company statement of its own
payload capacity is *not adjudication*; it is the same evidence class as the original claim,
better evidenced. See §6.

### 2.2 Channel O — the reference orbit. FIRES, +50.0%, period-independent.

Third-party sources give Electron's SSO capacity as **~200 kg** against 300 kg to a low-
inclination LEO. The thesis's denominator is "to LEO"; the missions actually flown include
SSO. At 200 kg:

```
  basis B:  $4.4M / 200 kg = $22,000/kg   vs $14,667/kg  →  +50.0%
  basis A:  $9.1M / 200 kg = $45,500/kg   vs $30,333/kg  →  +50.0%
```

**This channel is not closed by the filed record — it is contradicted by it.** The 10-K's
"38 to 120 degrees" envelope *encompasses* SSO, so the filing asserts 300 kg capacity across
the SSO band that third parties price at ~200 kg. That is a live filed-vs-third-party
conflict. It cannot be adjudicated here: the 200 kg figure is not admissible under §1b
(no government manifest, no filing, no contract), and the filed figure is admissible but
is an issuer self-assertion. **Reported as an unresolvable conflict, named, not resolved.**

### 2.3 Channel M — the HASTE mission mix. Partially filed; fires depending on the period.

This is the channel 001 could not see, because it is disclosed on a page 001 did not read.

> **`sec109` p.37** — "**Two of the six Electron launch missions completed for the three
> months ended June 30, 2026 were Hypersonic Accelerator Suborbital Test Electron
> ('HASTE') launch missions**, for which revenue was recognized over time and was partially
> recognized in prior quarters. All five Electron launch missions completed for the three
> months ended June 30, 2025 were point-in-time launches."
> [📄 RKLB 10-Q p.37](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/37)

HASTE is "a suborbital testbed launch vehicle derived from Rocket Lab's heritage Electron
rocket" (`sec87` p.8) — it is filed **inside the Launch Services section, beside Electron**,
and it **delivers 0 kg to LEO**. The issuer's disclosed per-launch metric divides by all six
missions. **One third of the Q2 2026 denominator delivered no payload to orbit.**

**The algebra.** Let n = missions in the period, m = of which HASTE; let C be the disclosed
blended per-launch figure and X the figure the metric attributes to each HASTE mission. The
$/kg the thesis wants uses only the (n − m) orbital missions, so:

```
  O = (n·C − m·X) / (n − m)
  Δ = O/C − 1 = [ m / (n − m) ] · ( 1 − X/C )
```

The `m/(n−m)` term is the whole story: **the channel's exposure is the HASTE share of the
period's missions.** Firing condition, |Δ| > 15%:

```
  X/C  outside  [ 1 − 0.15·(n−m)/m ,  1 + 0.15·(n−m)/m ]
```

| Period | m / n | HASTE share | Fires iff HASTE per-mission figure is outside |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q2 2026** | 2 / 6 | **33.3%** | **[70.0%, 130.0%] of the blended average** |
| **H1 2026** | ≥2 / 12, count undisclosed | **≤16.7%** | **[25.0%, 175.0%] of the blended average** |

Evaluated on the disclosed averages:

| | Basis B (cost) | Basis A (price) |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosed blended (Q2 2026) | $4.4M | $9.1M |
| **Q2 firing window** | **[$3.08M, $5.72M]** | **[$6.37M, $11.83M]** |
| Band edges at ±15% | +15.0% at $3.08M · −15.0% at $5.72M | +15.0% at $6.37M · −15.0% at $11.83M |
| Corner at X = 0 | **+50.0%** → $22,000/kg | **+50.0%** → $45,500/kg |
| Disclosed blended (H1 2026) | $4.9M | $9.2M |
| **H1 firing window (m=2)** | **[$1.23M, $8.58M]** | **[$2.30M, $16.10M]** |

**Reading:** the *quarterly* figure is far more exposed than the *half-yearly*, because the
channel scales with HASTE's share of the mission count, which is diluted 12-month-wards.
On Q2 2026 the basis-B window is tight — a HASTE mission costing less than 70% of an Electron
fires the falsifier. On H1 2026 it would take a HASTE cost below 25% of the blended average,
which is **not credible** for a vehicle sharing Electron's stages. **The H1 basis-B figure
($16,333/kg) is plausibly robust to channel M; the Q2 figure ($14,667/kg) is not.**

**The H1 count is undisclosed and cannot be recovered.** `sec109` p.37 gives the H1 narrative
with no HASTE mission count; [`sec104` p.31](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec104/31) states
Q1 2026 recognised "revenue on over-time HASTE launch missions" and likewise gives
**no count**. So the record establishes **m ≥ 2 of 12** and no upper bound. **Channel M is half-open in the filed record — and the open half is
the half that fires.**

### 2.4 Channel S — non-launch revenue inside the Launch Services line.

`sec109` p.37 discloses Q2 2026 "increased **other launch revenue of $5.7 million**, which
includes contract termination and study revenue" — inside Launch Services, but not revenue
attributable to a launch contract performance obligation. Against Q2 Launch Services revenue
of $44,586k that is **~13% of the segment line**, and it moves in the wrong direction to
close §3's reconciliation gap. Any use of the segment table to cross-check the per-launch
metric carries a ±13%-class composition uncertainty before any denominator question arises.

### 2.5 Combined, Q2 2026 corner

Channels O and M are independent and multiplicative: `Δ_total = (1+Δ_O)(1+Δ_M) − 1`.

```
  O = 200 kg (SSO)  and  X = 0 (HASTE attributed nothing):
  basis B:  (1.5)(1.5) − 1 = +125.0%   →  $14,667  →  $33,000/kg
  basis A:  (1.5)(1.5) − 1 = +125.0%   →  $30,333  →  $68,250/kg
```

A corner, not a forecast — but it bounds the space. **Both figures remain 25–33× PIL-1's
$1,000/kg threshold.**

## 3. DA-01: basis C is not constructible for RKLB, and the filing says why

The §1c standing rule requires every competing basis to be reported. DA-01 has three:
(A) customer list price, (B) marginal cost per launch, (C) fully-loaded amortized.

**Basis C cannot be built for RKLB, and this is a filed fact, not a limitation of effort:**

> **`sec109` p.33** — "Management does not regularly review either reporting segment's total
> assets or operating expenses. This is because in general, the Company's long-lived assets,
> facilities, and equipment are shared by each reporting segment."
> [📄 RKLB 10-Q p.33](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/sec109/33)

Segment operating expense is not reviewed, therefore not allocated, therefore not disclosed.
Basis C would require allocating shared R&D, SG&A and facilities to Launch Services; the
issuer does not, and its stated reason is physical asset sharing. **Basis C is unavailable
for every RKLB period, permanently, by structure.** This is a stronger statement than "not
attempted" and it should be recorded against DA-01 for the whole universe sweep: for a
vertically integrated launch-plus-components issuer, basis C is not a disclosure that can
be waited for.

Bases A and B remain for RKLB, and 001 reported both — satisfying `no_single_basis_collapse`.

**Consequence for PIL-1's falsifier design**: a ±15% test run against a *quarterly* RKLB
figure cannot distinguish a denominator restatement from the metric's own noise. The
registered falsifier is only discriminable on H1 or FY figures. This is a P7 finding.

## 5. Basis A is two-sided, and the only number that would close it is withheld

Every open channel on **basis B** is one-signed **upward** (capacity ≥ delivered; HASTE's
cost ≤ Electron's on any credible reading). Basis B therefore rests on a denominand that can
only make the $/kg too *low*. Fortifying.

**Basis A is not one-signed.** Because HASTE's transaction price exceeds Electron's on the
company's own framing — an analyst puts it at "I think HASTE, you're able to charge more",
uncontradicted by management
([📄 RKLB Q2 2026 call p.3](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/ect21/3)) — stripping it *lowers* the
blended orbital price:

```
  Δ_A = 0.5 − R_h/18.2        (Q2 2026)
  R_h = $9.1M  → Δ = 0.00%   → $30,333/kg
  R_h = $11.83M → Δ = −15.0% → $25,783/kg
  R_h = $14.8M (266/18) → Δ = −31.3% → $20,833/kg
```

**The ±15%-firing number is the one that circulates.** On the Q2 2026 call an analyst derived
"a $15 million sort of ASP" from the $266M / up-to-18-mission Space Force suborbital award,
and the CFO **confirmed it is contaminated** — "there is some funding in there for
infrastructure" — then **declined to give the HASTE ASP**, saying only that pricing is
"pretty consistent with what we have been showing on HASTE."
[📄 RKLB Q2 2026 call p.4](https://agentii.ai/v/RKLB/ect21/4)

So: the naive proxy ($266M/18 = $14.8M) sits **outside** basis A's firing window and would
restate the headline **−31.3%**; the CFO's own correction (infrastructure mixed in) lowers
the true launch ASP, which moves it *toward* the window; and the filed record contains no
HASTE price, cost, or margin at all. **Basis A's channel is live, material in both
directions, and not evaluable from the filed record.** Company-statement class throughout —
non-adjudicating, named for direction only.

**This is the only channel identified anywhere in this artifact that can move an Electron
headline DOWN** — and note that the registered falsifier's operator is **symmetric**
