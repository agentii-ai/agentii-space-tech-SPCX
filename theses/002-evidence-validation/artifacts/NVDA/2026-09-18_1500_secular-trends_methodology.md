## 1. The H100 thermal finding — mechanism corroborated, event still `CLAIMED`

### 1.1 What the platform does carry

Checked and exhausted: the H100 appears in NVDA's corpus in only two substantive
contexts — **rental pricing** and **export controls**. In the Q1 FY2027 call Jensen
Huang cites *"the price of renting an **H100** has risen 20% year to date"*
([📄 NVDA earnings_call_transcript p.1](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/ect81/1)) — a
terrestrial scarcity signal, not a thermal one. The only H100-bearing page in the

([📄 NVDA 10-Q p.36](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/sec173/36)).

**No H100 TDP, thermal limit, junction temperature, or power figure appears anywhere
in NVDA's filings or transcripts.** The platform carries no NVDA product datasheet.
"Starcloud" returns **zero** hits in `sec173`, `sec169` and `ect81`.

### 1.2 The find — the issuer's CEO answers the question directly

The Q4 FY2026 call carried a space-data-center question that 001 did not have.
Antoine Chkaiban (New Street Research) asked whether space data centers are feasible;
Jen-Hsun Huang answered
([📄 NVDA earnings_call_transcript p.4](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/ect80/4)):

> *"Well, the economics are poor today, but it's going to improve over time. ... The
> heat dissipation, it's cold in space. However, there's no airflow. And so the only
> way to dissipate heat is through conduction and the radiators that you need to
> create are fairly large. Liquid cooling is obviously out of the question because
> it's kind of — it's heavy and freezes. ... And so NVIDIA is already the world's
> first GPU in space, Hopper is in space."*

Four things follow, and the grading discipline matters more than the quotations.

**(a) F2's mechanism is confirmed by the vendor of the part — from the CEO's mouth.**
Not airflow, conduction only, radiators "fairly large" — this is F2's physics stated
independently of 001, by the counterparty with the most commercial interest in the
opposite being true. 001 called F2 "the named binding constraint"; the constraint's own
supplier agrees.

**(b) It supplies a mechanism for the H100 failure — and that is not confirmation of
it.** A terrestrial part with a terrestrial cooling design, in an environment with no
airflow, is exactly what would produce a cooling-limited derate. NVDA's *terrestrial*
roadmap has moved further in the wrong direction: its flagship is *"a data center scale,
**liquid-cooled** design"*
([📄 NVDA 10-K p.4](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/sec169/4)), and Jensen states liquid
cooling is "obviously out of the question" in orbit. **The terrestrial thermal roadmap
and the orbital requirement are diverging.** Plausibility raised; grade unchanged.

> ⚠️ **Mechanism corroboration is not event confirmation.** Starcloud is private; its
> flight report is press. Under Q-4 it is **`CLAIMED` only and can never satisfy a
> falsifier** — and NVDA's CEO, the one source on this platform positioned to confirm
> the derate, **confirms the flight and not the failure**. "Hopper is in space" is on
> the record; "cannot run at full power" is not. **The H100 thermal finding therefore
> remains `CLAIMED` — it does not move.**

**(c) The H100's numeric thermal limit is `UNRESOLVABLE-FROM-PLATFORM`.** The figure is
public (in NVIDIA's own datasheets, off-platform) but no instrument in this registry
reaches it, and no filing discloses a per-accelerator thermal figure. Named resolving
source: **an NVIDIA product datasheet, or a filed disclosure of per-accelerator TDP.**
Neither is carried. Same class as PIL-6 (public but unreachable) — not the same class as
PIL-4 (does not exist).

**(d) The generation in orbit is the one NVDA markets as obsolete on the metric NVDA
says matters.** Jensen names Hopper as the part in space. In the same call's prepared
remarks, Colette Kress positions GB300 NVL72 at *"up to 50x performance per watt and
35x lower cost per token compared with [ Hopper ]"*
([📄 NVDA earnings_call_transcript p.1](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/ect80/1)). The
orbital deployment sits a generation behind on **tokens per watt** — the exact metric
NVDA's CEO calls "literally everything" for power-constrained buyers.

**One caution for downstream quoting.** Jensen also says *"There's an abundance of
energy"* in space. Read whole, he immediately qualifies it — *"but solar panels are
large"* — so F1 is not contradicted. Quoted alone, the fragment would misrepresent
NVDA as disputing F1. **Do not lift that clause.**

### 1.3 The CEO's stated use case is not the thesis's use case

This is the part 001 could not have had, and it is worth more than the absence finding.
Jensen's own orbital use case is **imaging**:

> *"one of the best use cases of GPUs in space is imaging, to be able to image at
> extremely high resolutions ... It's hard to do that by sending petabytes and
> petabytes of imaging data back here on earth and doing that work. It's easier just
> to do it out in space. And then ignore all of the data collected and processed until

That is **edge inference for downlink reduction** — a low-power, small-form-factor

indifference from an absence; the Q4 call states the company's actual orbital thesis,
and it is a different product category from the one the sector narrative is built on.
Grade `CLAIMED` (management assertion, unquantified) — but it converts 001's *inferred*
revealed preference into a *stated* one.

## 3. The cost stack — the claim is challenged, and by 001's own source

> 001, §2: *"the GPU is **not** the dominant cost term. The constraint is, and NVDA
> sells into a market where its own product is the smaller line item."*

**This contradicts the artifact it cites.** 001's own Phase 2 cross artifact says the
opposite in terms:

> `theses/001-technology-baseline/_cross/phase-2-constraint-envelope.md` §Corrections 3:
> *"Power+thermal is ~$2.3–4.6M/MW of launch cost against a reported $10–40M/MW all-in —
> meaningful, not dominant. **The dominant term is the compute hardware and its
> replacement rate, which this phase did not model.**"*

The NVDA artifact cites Phase 2 in support of a proposition Phase 2 expressly rejects.
**The "GPU is a minor line" reading inverts its own source**, and 001's carry-forward 3
("Phase 6 should present the orbital-compute cost stack with the GPU as a *minor* line")
propagates the inversion into 003–011. Grade `DEMONSTRATED` — a text-level fact about
two artifacts, verifiable by reading both.

**The arithmetic, on figures already in the workspace.** Phase 2's own numbers give a
share of the all-in figure attributable to *launching* the power+thermal mass of:

| power+thermal launch | all-in $10M/MW | all-in $40M/MW |
|---|---|---|
| $2.3M/MW | **23.0%** | 5.75% |
| $4.6M/MW | **46.0%** | 11.5% |

"Meaningful but not dominant" is defensible only in the upper half of the all-in band.
**At the $10M/MW corner, launching the power+thermal mass alone is 46% of all-in — and
that is a transport line excluding the array, the radiator and the reactor hardware, and
excluding the GPU entirely.** The verdict is a corner-dependent one presented as
corner-independent.

**Why the GPU term is absent, and why that is decisive.** `DA-25` — no GPU ASP, price,
or $/MW appears in any NVDA filing. NVDA is not required to disclose it and does not.
So the claim *"the GPU is not the dominant cost term"* was reached **without ever
placing a GPU term in the table**. That is not a refutation; it is an **unmodelled
denominator**, and it is the largest single line in a terrestrial AI factory.

**The evidence that does exist points the other way.** NVDA's GAAP gross margin is
**74.9%** ([📄 p.1](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/ect81/1)) / **75%**
([📄 p.1](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/ect80/1)) and its operating margin 65.6%
([📄 p.3](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/sec173/3)). A 75% gross margin means the buyer's
silicon line is roughly **4× NVDA's cost of revenue by construction** — the margin
measures the seller's rent, not the buyer's cost share. Reading a high margin as
evidence the part is *cheap to the buyer* inverts the sign of the inference.

**What does hold — and it is a different claim.** The *direction* is confirmed by the
vendor, twice, unprompted: *"Today's data centers are revenue generating AI factories
**constrained by power and capital**"* and *"Customers do not buy GPUs. They build AI
factories"* ([📄 p.1](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/ect81/1)); *"Every data center is
**power-constrained**. Customers make critical architectural decisions based on
performance per watt"* ([📄 p.1](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/ect80/1)). NVDA denominates
its own installed base in **MW** — *"partner data centers exceeding 10MW ... surpassing
80 sites"* (ect81 p.1), *"nearly 9 gigawatts of infrastructure on Blackwell are
deployed"* (ect80 p.1) — not in GPU counts. Its 10-Q names energy as a gating input:
*"Expanding energy capacity to meet demand is a complex, multi-year process"*
([📄 NVDA 10-Q p.23](https://agentii.ai/v/NVDA/sec173/23)).

> **Confirmed: power is the binding *constraint*. Not confirmed, and challenged: that
> compute is the smaller *cost share*.** Those are different claims and the sector
> narrative slides between them. The constraint binds the *buyer's* siting decision;
> cost share is an arithmetic question no issuer in this universe discloses.

### 4.2 What the literature says, when reached directly (NOT platform-resolvable)

Reached by web search only. **These are not read-verified citations, carry no
`citation_id`, and are recorded here as leads for `_cross/f2-constant-sourcing.md` —
not as validated figures.** Grade: `CLAIMED` (secondary survey, not a read paper).

**Constant 1 — radiator areal density.** Reported values cluster as:

| Class | kg/m² |
|---|---|
| Advanced / low-mass (carbon-carbon, SAFE-400, C-C flat plate) | **1.0 – 1.6** |
| Structural panels (Al alloy, ISS honeycomb, beryllium, carbon fibre) | 2.44 – 3.0 |
| Flown ISS radiator ORU (6,492 kg / 951 m²) | **~6.8** |
| Crewed / ISS-type, JAXA prototype, Chinese-station limit | **5.3 – 11** |

**Constant 2 — heat-pump COP at elevated rejection temperature. This is the finding,
and it attacks F2's headline rather than just its error bar.** The literature does not
supply "a COP at 500 K"; it supplies a **mass optimum at a much lower temperature**:

- COP is Carnot-limited and falls as lift rises; lower COP raises the **heat ratio**
  (heat rejected ÷ load heat), so total heat to be rejected grows and *"the full area
  reduction cannot be realized"*.
- *"There is consequently an **optimum rejection temperature**, not the highest
  attainable one."*
- Reported optima, for real systems at 300 K-class loads: **~355 K** (55 K lift, ~35%
  area reduction, ~15% mass saving at 10 kg/m² radiator specific mass) and, for a
  lunar-base study with the heat pump at **40–60% of Carnot**, **374–387 K**.
- 500 K and above is described as a *development* requirement — lightweight heat pumps
  and heat engines operating above 500 K — with multi-megawatt rejection "expected at
  1000 K and above", i.e. **not a demonstrated design point for a 300 K-class load.**

**So 001's 500 K column is not merely optimistic on COP. It sits past the mass optimum
the literature identifies.**

### 4.3 Closing the loop 001 left open — the 24× becomes 9.6×–16.8×

cold-side temperature, which 001 itself states at *"chip-junction temperatures
(~350–400 K)"* (`phase-2-constraint-envelope.md` line 61). For a heat pump,
`COP = Q_hot/W` and `Q_hot = Q_c + W`, so the heat ratio — the factor by which radiator
area is multiplied — is **`f = Q_hot/Q_c = 1/(1 − 1/COP)`**, with
`COP_Carnot = T_r/(T_r − T_c)`.

For `T_c = 350 K → T_r = 500 K`: `COP_Carnot = 500/150 = 3.33`. In the **nuclear**
configuration (no array, so the radiator is the only area term):

| Case | COP | `f` | Radiator m²/MW | vs 300 K |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 K, no pump (001's baseline) | — | 1.00 | 2,419 | 1.00× |
| 500 K, no pump (**001's claim**) | ∞ | 1.00 | 313 | **7.72×** |
| 500 K, Carnot (**unattainable bound**) | 3.33 | 1.43 | 447 | 5.41× |
| 500 K, 50% of Carnot (**realistic**) | 1.67 | **2.50** | **783** | **3.09×** |

**001's headline 24× reduction decomposes as `3.10× (array removal) × 7.72× (300→500 K)`.
Unpriced, the two compound. Priced, they do not.** Substituting the achievable factors:
`3.10 × 5.41 = 16.8×` (Carnot) and `3.10 × 3.09 = 9.6×` (realistic 50%-Carnot).
**The nuclear case is 9.6×–16.8×, not 24×.**

In the **solar** configuration the pump's power is paid in *array* area, and the array is
the dominant term (5,080 m²/MW vs 2,420). At `f`=2.5 the electrical load rises 2.5×; the
array goes to 12,700 m²/MW and the radiator to 783 m²/MW — total **13,483 vs 7,499 =
1.80× WORSE than solar at 300 K.** At 60% of Carnot (`f`=3.0) it is 2.16× worse.
**Raising rejection temperature is self-defeating without nuclear**, because the solar
architecture pays the pump penalty in array area roughly **16× over** what nuclear pays
it in radiator area. The pump's power draw is not a rounding error; **it decides the
sign.**
