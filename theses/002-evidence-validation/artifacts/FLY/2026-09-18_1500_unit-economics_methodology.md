**And "1,000 kilograms" is not a point even on a generous reading.** The same 10-K page
that uses the class language reports that the market *"right-sized toward satellites
between **200 kilograms to 1,200 kilograms**"* and that Alpha serves *"the majority of
these payloads"* — a 6× span. "Class" language denotes a segment, not a capability.

> **Consequence for FLY's own unit economics: no $/kg is computable for Alpha on any
> basis.** Not because the cost side is missing (it is — §2), but because the
> *denominator* does not exist as a number in FLY's filings either.

### 1a. A method finding for P1, surfaced by the supporting case

Spec §1b admits a source if it is *"a government manifest, a filed document, or a
customer contract"*, and rules out *"a company webpage stating its own payload
capacity"*.

**FLY's payload statement is inside a filed document — and it is still not
adjudication.** It is the issuer describing its own product in its own marketing
register ("class-leading", "the only U.S. company"). A literal reading of the
document-class test would admit it and let FLY **self-adjudicate its own denominator**.

**Proposed refinement for the P1 owner: the admissibility test needs an *independence*
clause, not only a document-class clause.** "Filed" is necessary but not sufficient; the
figure must originate outside the vehicle's own manufacturer. Not adopted unilaterally —
flagged, because the same trap applies to the three real denominators, each of which is
a manufacturer's own claim.

## 2. The DA-06 absence, confirmed from the filing

**Finding.** *FLY discloses no per-launch cost figure and no per-launch revenue figure
anywhere in the Q2 2026 Form 10-Q.* Pages 1, 7, 15–16, 33, 34–44 and 50 were read in
full. `cost per launch`, `revenue per launch`, `launch cost`, `cost per` and `number of
launches` were each run through `search_keyword_in_source`; **no page in any returned
set contains the metric when read.**

**This matters because it converts 001's assertion into a cited absence.** 001 recorded
that FLY discloses neither. 001 was right. But the claim that carried forward into the
workspace — *basis B's demonstrated anchor is single-source* — rested on an absence that
had not been demonstrated from a filing. It is now.

### 2a. Four structural reasons the absence is durable, not incidental

This is the part that makes the finding load-bearing rather than a gap to be filled
later. Each is independently cited.

1. **There is no "Key Business Metrics" section to hold such a metric.**
   `search_keyword_in_source(sec16, "key metrics")` returns **zero hits** across the
   entire annual report. RKLB's per-launch figures live in exactly such a section; FLY
   has no section of that kind in either filing. The metric is not merely unstated — it
   has no home in FLY's disclosure architecture.
2. **FLY is a single reportable segment**, so there is no launch-segment cost table to
   divide either. *"We operate as a single reportable segment"*
   ([10-Q p.34](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/34); [10-K p.60](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec16/60)).
   The CODM table at [p.33](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/33) gives **consolidated**
   cost of sales, R&D and SG&A only. **DA-25-style reconciliation is structurally
   impossible at FLY** — there is no audited segment table to reconcile a normalised
   metric against, on either side of the comparison.
3. **The Launch revenue line is definitionally mixed, so it cannot substitute.** *"Launch
   revenue includes revenues from contracts … to provide launch and integration services
   … We also enter into contracts with our customers to provide **engineering services,
   including the development of launch sites** and related components, and to **develop
   and provide licenses to intellectual property**"*
   ([p.38](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/38)). Three different revenue types share one
   line. Even if a launch count existed, revenue ÷ launches would not be a launch price.
   The revenue-recognition policy confirms the mixing is not a labelling artefact: the
   launch performance obligation is the **initiation of the launch**, recognised at a
   point in time, with multi-element stand-alone selling prices set by *"cost-plus-margin
   or market prices"* ([p.16](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/16)) — so the line contains
   an allocation of a bundled price, not an observed price. The ASC 606 disaggregation
   table at [p.15](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/15) is the **only** revenue split FLY
   provides, and it is this same two-line split — disaggregation adds no resolution below
   it.
4. **No launch count is disclosed either.** The only launch named in the entire H1 2026
   discussion is **Alpha Flight 7** ([p.42](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/42)). There is
   no "number of launches" metric in either filing.

**And point 3 is not hypothetical — it is what actually happened in the quarter.** Q2
2026 Launch revenue grew $3.1M (+48%) to $9.4M, attributed *"primarily due to our
progress on **engineering services contracts for the development of launch facilities**"*
([p.40](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/40)). **The growth in FLY's Launch line is not
launch services at all.** A reader who treated Launch revenue as a launch-price proxy
would be reading launch-pad construction revenue as rocket revenue.

### 2b. Consequence for PIL-1

| Basis | FLY discloses? | SPCX discloses? | RKLB discloses? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** — customer list price | ✗ | price/launch `CLAIMED` | ✓ `DEMONSTRATED` |
| **A-prime** | ✗ | `DEMONSTRATED` (per 001) | ✗ |
| **B** — marginal cost per launch | ✗ | ✗ | **✓ `DEMONSTRATED`** |
| **C** — fully-loaded amortized | ✗ | `DEMONSTRATED` (per 001) | ✗ |
| `cost per launch` metric | ✗ | ✗ | **✓ only issuer** |
| `revenue per launch` metric | ✗ | ✗ | **✓ only issuer** |

Per the §1c standing rule, **all four bases are reported as absent at FLY** — no basis is
collapsed into another and none is estimated.

**Verdict: basis B's demonstrated anchor is single-source, and now confirmed so by
construction rather than by assertion.** The demonstrated marginal cost in the universe
is Electron/RKLB — $14,667/kg on basis B, $30,333/kg on basis A (001's
`RKLB/…_unit-economics_methodology.md`; cited, not recomputed). Neither remaining
manufacturer can widen it. The SPCX half of the same claim is 001's and is restated
here by reference to the frozen 001 artifact, per §1d.

**What this does to P1's *method*.** P1 can still succeed — it may resolve the three
denominators from government manifests or customer contracts, which are the admissible
sources §1b names. But **P1 cannot expect to *widen the demonstrated cost anchor* by
adding issuers**, and a P1 write-up that implies basis B will strengthen once more
filers are examined would be planning around something that cannot happen. Basis B
versus basis A stays a one-issuer comparison.

**`EPS × shares` was not used, and at FLY it is disqualified on two independent

1. **It shares the flip.** 001 recorded that FLY **passes** the test *while being

2. **The capital structure beneath it is discontinuous.** The same page shows
   weighted-average shares of **161,784K** in Q2 2026 against **13,877K** in Q2 2025 —
   an 11.6× step from the August 2025 IPO ([p.6](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/6)).

   ($(5.78) → $(0.57)). This is the **DA-28 class** (IPO capital-structure
   discontinuity) that P3 registered at HAWK, appearing at FLY as well.

Per the contract and §1c its use is a defect, not a shortcut.

### 3a. Two leads for P3, recorded because this instrument produced them

Out of scope for PIL-1; recorded so the P3 owner does not have to rediscover them.

**(i) `validate_calculation` returned `pass` on a sign-stripped parent.** The cash flow
statement at [p.7](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/7) reports *"Net cash used in investing
activities $(119,780)"* and *"Net (decrease) … in cash and cash equivalents and
restricted cash $(333,149)"*. The XBRL extract reports **+119,780,000** and
**+333,149,000** — both stripped. The instrument flags the first (`NetCashProvidedByUsedInInvestingActivities`, computed −119,780,000 vs reported +119,780,000, **FAIL**) but
records `CashCashEquivalentsRestrictedCashAndRestrictedCashEquivalentsPeriodIncreaseDecreaseIncludingExchangeRateEffect` as computed **333,149,000** = reported **333,149,000**, **PASS**.
The reason is structural: the calculation tree sums its children, the children carry the
same inversion, and the parent therefore *computes* the flipped value and matches.
**A `pass` from the arc layer is not evidence of a correct sign.** This is a
detector-availability limitation, not an issuer defect, and it bears directly on how
P3's remedy is applied.

**(ii) The same extract carries 9 fails of 21 tested concepts** on this one filing, of
which 2 are the `OperatingIncomeLoss` inversions and one is the investing-activities
flip above; the remainder are level or tree mismatches (e.g. `PropertyPlantAndEquipmentNet`
computed −45,145,000 against a reported 186,057,000) whose causes are **not adjudicated
here**. Recorded as a P3 lead, not a claim.

prior three years; or (iv) being deemed a large accelerated filer. FLY's H1 2026 revenue
is $198.6M ([p.42](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/42)) — **~$397M annualised against a
$1.235B trigger**, so (i) is far off; the IPO completed 2025-08-08, so (ii) runs to
roughly FY2030. **EGC status persists for years.**

### What EGC does NOT excuse — and this is the load-bearing point

**The per-launch absence is not an EGC accommodation.** EGC relief covers (a) extended
transition periods for *new accounting standards*, and (b) reduced compensation
disclosure and exemption from SOX 404(b) auditor attestation. It does **not** relax
Item 303 MD&A, ASC 280 segment reporting, ASC 606 revenue disaggregation, or the
obligation to file full financial statements. **RKLB's per-launch figures are a
voluntary key-operating-metric disclosure** — nothing compelled them, and nothing
forbids FLY from making the same one.

**Therefore: the absence is a voluntary-disclosure choice, not a regulatory
forbearance.** It will not close on any compliance calendar. Basis B stays single-source
until RKLB is joined by another *willing* filer, which is a business decision, not a
regulatory milestone. P1 and its downstream consumers should treat the single-source
anchor as **permanent, not transitional**.

**One more point in the same direction, and it is stronger than it first looks.**
Dropping the smaller-reporting-company box matters: FLY is **not** an SRC. It files full
financial statements and full MD&A. Had SRC status been marked, scaled disclosure would
have explained part of the thinness. **It is not marked**, so the thinness cannot be
attributed to scaled disclosure at all. A full-disclosure filer is choosing not to
disclose its per-unit launch economics.

### What EGC DOES affect — and it bounds what can be validated at FLY

EGC's real bite here is **comparability, not completeness**, and the filing says so
itself ([p.50](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/50)):

> *"…we, as an emerging growth company, may adopt the new or revised standard at the time
> private companies are required to adopt … This may make it difficult or impossible to
> compare our financial results with the financial results of another public company
> that is either not an emerging growth company or is an emerging growth company that has
> chosen not to take advantage of the extended transition period…"*

**Consequences, stated as a boundary on this artifact:**

| Validatable at FLY — EGC does not reach it | Bounded by EGC |
|---|---|
| The component identity / DA-23 (pure arithmetic on filed lines) | Any cross-issuer **margin or trend** comparison that depends on a recently issued standard — FLY's adoption **date** must be named or the comparison is uncontrolled |
| Revenue disaggregation, backlog, filing status, EGC status itself | Disclosure *richness* comparisons across the universe — FLY's thinness is a choice, but its *standard-timing* differences are not |
| The absence finding in §2 (an absence is not an accounting-standard question) | — |

**The asymmetry is observable, not theoretical.** From the standards note at
[pp.11–12](https://agentii.ai/v/FLY/sec21/12): FLY **early-adopted** ASU 2025-06
(internal-use software) effective **2026-01-01**, a standard whose ordinary effective
date is fiscal years beginning after **2027-12-15** — a ~2-year divergence from a
non-EGC peer. It **elected** the ASU 2025-05 credit-loss practical expedient from
**2026-01-01**. Meanwhile ASU 2024-03 (expense disaggregation) is **not adopted** *and
not yet required* — *"currently evaluating the impact"* — with an ordinary effective
date of annual periods beginning after 2026-12-15, and ASU 2025-11 (interim reporting)
is not effective until 2028. **Any cross-issuer expense-category or credit-loss
comparison involving FLY in this window is comparing different accounting bases.**

**So FLY remains a legitimate control/confirmation datum in a supporting role.** The
DA-23 confirmation in §3 is unaffected by EGC status, and the §2 absence finding is
*strengthened* by it, because a non-SRC filer with full disclosure obligations declining
to disclose per-launch economics is a different fact from a scaled filer omitting it.

**Correction to 001, recorded per the §1c frozen-file policy.** 001's FLY artifact
introduced the EGC status as *"a structural reason its filings reveal less than RKLB's"*.
That is **half right and misleading as stated.** EGC is a structural reason for reduced
*accounting-standard* comparability and reduced *governance* disclosure. It is **not** a
structural reason for the missing per-launch metrics — that absence is voluntary and
would be voluntary for a large accelerated filer too. 001's file is not rewritten
(spec §1c); the correction is recorded here and cross-cited.
