## 3. The eclipse-multiplier correction — the one F2 term that *does* move, and by exactly 1.587×

### 3.1 F1's chain contains a term that a dawn-dusk orbit deletes

F1's array derivation is a four-step chain, and **one step is orbit-specific**:

```
1 MW continuous, cell-level BOL 30%        1,000,000 / (1,361 x 0.30)     = 2,449 m2
  / eclipse duty (0.63 sunlight @ ~500 km)          x 1.587               = 3,886 m2   <-- ORBIT-DEPENDENT
  / array packing factor (0.85)                     / 0.85                = 4,572 m2
  / end-of-life degradation (0.90)                  / 0.90                = 5,080 m2
```

**The 1.587× eclipse multiplier is the only term in the chain that a different orbit
changes.** F1's 0.63 sunlight fraction is a **~500 km mid-inclination LEO** figure. Suncatcher's
stated advantage is *"the right orbit"* — and the right orbit for continuous compute is a
**dawn-dusk sun-synchronous orbit**, which is chosen precisely because it holds the satellite
in **continuous sunlight with no eclipse season**.

**Correcting for dawn-dusk SSO — and only for that:**

| Term | Eclipse LEO (F1) | **Dawn-dusk SSO** | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Solar array per MW | 5,080 m² | **3,201 m²** | **÷1.587** |
| Radiator @ 300 K | 2,419 m² | 2,419 m² | unchanged |
| **Combined @ 300 K** | **7,499 m²** | **5,620 m²** | −25.1% |
| Radiator @ 500 K | 313 m² | 313 m² | unchanged |
| **Combined @ 500 K** | 5,393 m² | **3,514 m²** | −34.8% |

**The orbit choice is worth exactly 1.587× on the array and nothing on the radiator.** That
is the entire first-principles content of Suncatcher's advantage that F1 can adjudicate, and
it is a **real 25–35% reduction in deployed area per MW** — not a rounding error, but an
order of magnitude short of "8×."

### 3.2 The "8×" claim — falsifiable only once its missing denominator is reconstructed

Suncatcher's claim, as 001 recorded it, is *"Solar panels up to 8× more productive in the
right orbit."* **The claim states no reference terrestrial capacity factor**, which 001
flagged. That gap can now be closed in the other direction: **invert the claim to find the
denominator it implies.**

```
Orbital, dawn-dusk SSO, F1's own chain:
  1,361 W/m2 (AM0)  x  0.30 (cell BOL)  x  0.85 (packing)  x  0.90 (EOL)   =  312.4 W/m2
  capacity factor ~ 1.00  (no eclipse)                     ->  312.4 W/m2 average

Terrestrial reference:
  1,000 W/m2 (AM1.5)  x  ~0.21 (module efficiency)  x  CF_t             =  210 x CF_t

  claimed multiple = 312.4 / (210 x CF_t) = 1.487 / CF_t

  ==>  8x  requires  CF_t = 1.487 / 8 = 18.6%
```

**The claim implies a reference terrestrial capacity factor of 18.6%, and that is a
checkable, plausible number** — at or just below the US utility-scale PV fleet (~20–24% in
recent years) and above the global average. **So the "8×" is neither confirmed nor refuted;
it is parameterised.**

| Assumed terrestrial CF | Implied multiple | Verdict on "8×" |
|---:|---:|---|
| 16% | 9.3× | 8× is **conservative** |
| **18.6%** | **8.0×** | **the claim's implicit reference** |
| 20% | 7.4× | 8× is **optimistic by ~8%** |
| 25% | 5.9× | 8× is **optimistic by ~35%** |

**This is the honest disposition and it is the opposite of a debunk.** The claim is
**directionally supported** — a dawn-dusk SSO array genuinely produces more energy per unit
area than a terrestrial array, for three independent and additive reasons: **no eclipse**
(1.587×, F1's own term), **higher irradiance** (1,361 vs 1,000 W/m² = 1.361×), and **the
terrestrial capacity factor** (1/CF_t ≈ 5×). Multiplied: **1.587 × 1.361 × 5.4 ≈ 11.7×** at
a 20% CF before any efficiency difference — so **a multiple of 8 is not merely reachable, it
is modest.** But **F1/F2 cannot falsify it**, because at a stated CF the arithmetic is
trivially satisfied and at an unstated CF the claim has no truth value.

**Reported as `MODELED`, and its inputs are named rather than hidden.** The 21% terrestrial
module efficiency is an assumption, not a sourced constant — **no solar-module efficiency is
carried anywhere on this platform class** — and per §1d a `MODELED` derivation cites its
*inputs*: F1's 1,361 W/m², 0.30 cell efficiency, 0.85 packing and 0.90 EOL are 001's
`_cross/phase-2-constraint-envelope.md`; the 0.63 sunlight fraction that the correction
deletes is from the same source.

## 4. The acceptance test fires — F2 downgrades to a qualitative bound

`spec.md` §PIL-2 sets the acceptance test explicitly: *"if the sourced band exceeds ±50%,
F2 downgrades to a qualitative bound — state that 003 and 009 must be notified."* The
falsifier is `metric=f2_radiator_mass_per_MW_uncertainty_band_pct threshold=0.50
source=peer_reviewed_literature_or_flown_hardware_disclosure op=>`.

### 4.1 The band, term by term — and it does not close

| Term | Value / range | Sourced? | Band contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radiator area @ 300 K** | 2,419 m²/MW | 001 MODELLED | — |
| **Radiator area @ 500 K** | 313 m²/MW | 001 MODELLED | **parameter range 7.72×** |
| **Radiator areal density** | **8 kg/m²** | ❌ **admitted placeholder** | **unbounded — no sourced value exists on this platform** |
| **Heat-pump COP at 400–500 K** | **not applied** | ❌ **absent** | **one-sided +33% to +100%** on rejected heat |
| Array eclipse term | ×1.587 or ×1.000 | 001 MODELLED | 1.587×, orbit-dependent |
| Array packing factor | 0.85 | 001 MODELLED | ±10% |
| Array EOL degradation | 0.90 over mission life | 001 MODELLED | ±5% over plausible life |

**The band cannot be closed, and the reason is structural rather than quantitative.**
P2's falsifier names its admissible source class: **`peer_reviewed_literature_or_flown_hardware_disclosure`.**
**This platform carries neither.** There is no peer-reviewed journal in the corpus, and no
issuer in the universe discloses a spacecraft thermal subsystem mass — `VRT` is a
*terrestrial* thermal comparator whose 10-Q contains no radiator areal density, and 001's own
cross artifact already recorded that **8 kg/m² "is a placeholder. Phase 3 should attempt to
source it."** Three artifacts later, it is still a placeholder.

**So the band is not ±X%. It is undefined** — one of its two factors has no value at all.
**An undefined band exceeds ±50% by construction**, and F2's acceptance test therefore fires
**not marginally but categorically.**

### 4.2 What the downgrade does and does not do

**Radiator mass per MW, on every basis this platform can construct:**

| Basis | Area/MW | Areal density | **Mass/MW** | Heat-pump penalty |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 300 K, placeholder density | 2,419 m² | 8 kg/m² | **19.4 t/MW** | none (no pump needed) |
| 500 K, placeholder density, **no pump** — 001's figure | 313 m² | 8 kg/m² | **2.5 t/MW** | **omitted** |
| 500 K, **with COP = 2** | 470 m² *(313 × 1.5)* | 8 kg/m² | **3.8 t/MW** | **+50% rejected heat** |
| 500 K, **with COP = 1** | 626 m² *(313 × 2.0)* | 8 kg/m² | **5.0 t/MW** | **+100% rejected heat** |
| 300 K, density range 5–15 kg/m² | 2,419 m² | 5–15 | **12.1–36.3 t/MW** | none |

**Two conclusions survive the downgrade, and they are the ones that matter:**

1. **F2's qualitative bound is robust.** One MW of continuous orbital load requires **order
   10³ m² of deployed surface** and **order 10¹ tonnes of power-and-thermal mass**. That
   statement is unchanged by a 7.72× area range, a 3× density range, or a 2× COP penalty.
   **The constraint still binds — it just cannot be quoted to four significant figures.**
2. **The nuclear case is overstated in 001 and understated in the sector's rhetoric, and the
   COP penalty is why.** 001's headline is *"313 m²/MW, a 24× reduction."* **With a COP = 2
   heat pump folded in, the honest figure is 470 m²/MW and a ~16× reduction** — and the
   array term it eliminates (5,080 m²/MW) is untouched, so the 24× is a *total-area* claim
   that survives only if the pump is free. **It is not free, and 001 never closed the loop.**

**⚠️ Disposition, stated plainly as the acceptance test requires: 003 and 009 must be
notified that F2 is downgraded to a qualitative bound.** Any of their figures quoted to
three or four significant figures off the 7,499 / 2,419 / 313 m² set **must be restated as
order-of-magnitude**, and the 24× nuclear case **must be restated with a named, non-zero COP
or labelled an upper bound.** The upgrade path is unchanged and specific: **a peer-reviewed
or flown-hardware radiator areal density, and a heat-pump COP at the stated rejection
temperature.** Both are absent from this platform by class, not by search failure (§9).
